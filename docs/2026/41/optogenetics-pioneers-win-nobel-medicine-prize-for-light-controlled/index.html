Optogenetics pioneers win Nobel medicine prize for light-controlled neurons

Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel share the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering a light-activated molecular switch that lets scientists turn individual nerve cells on and off.

The Nobel Assembly at Sweden's Karolinska Institute on Monday gave the 2026 Prize in Physiology or Medicine to three scientists whose work made it possible to control brain cells with light. The winners are Karl Deisseroth, 54, of Stanford University in the United States, Peter Hegemann, 71, of Humboldt University of Berlin, and Georg Nagel, 73, of the University of Würzburg. The assembly cited them for discovering a molecular switch for nerve cells. They share 12 million Swedish crowns, about $1.2 million.

The discovery began with pond algae. In the early 1990s, at a Max Planck Institute, Hegemann studied how the single-celled green alga Chlamydomonas swims towards light. Working with Nagel, he identified channelrhodopsin, a light-sensitive protein channel that opens almost instantly when light hits it and keeps working when placed in other cells. Deisseroth then put the gene for the protein into rat nerve cells, so that flashes of blue light could fire signals inside a living brain.

The technique, called optogenetics, lets researchers work out which types of cell drive behaviours such as learning, fear, addiction and movement, and how those cells connect. Committee chair Per Svenningsson said it allows the brain to be mapped in ways scientists could once only dream of. Thomas Perlmann, the assembly's secretary-general, said laboratories around the world now use it. Researchers are also exploring medical uses, including cochlear implants that could stimulate the hearing nerve more precisely than today's electrical devices.

The choice was not free of debate. STAT reported that many commentators on social media said Ed Boyden, first author of Deisseroth's landmark 2005 paper, should also have been recognised. Perlmann said the three laureates are close friends and were honoured to win together.

The medicine prize opened this year's Nobel week. Physics, chemistry, literature and peace follow, and all laureates will receive their awards on 10 December, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death.

Why it matters

Optogenetics is now a standard tool in neuroscience labs around the world and underpins research into brain disorders, addiction and possible new treatments.
