Conflict · Wednesday 7 October 2026

Russian missile barrage kills dozens in Ukraine, flattening Pryluky apartments

Seventy missiles and dozens of drones hit Ukraine overnight, and a strike on a five-storey building in Pryluky killed 22 people, five of them children.

Russia launched one of its heaviest attacks of the war overnight into 7 October. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Russia fired 70 missiles, many of them fast ballistic weapons, along with dozens of Shahed drones. The strikes hit energy and industrial sites as well as homes. Zelenskiy put the national death toll at no fewer than 25, with about 100 wounded. Interior Minister Ivan Vyhivskyi gave a figure of 28.

The worst damage was in Pryluky, a town in the northern Chernihiv region. A cruise missile, identified by Meduza as a Kh-101, tore through a five-storey apartment block early in the morning while many residents were asleep. Rescuers used an excavator and shovels to search the rubble and brought 15 people down from the upper floors. By 8 October the town's death toll had reached 22, including five children, with 54 injured. Whole families were among the dead. Pryluky declared three days of mourning.

Deaths were also reported in Kyiv, Kremenchuk and Oleksandriia. The barrage came on Vladimir Putin's birthday. In his nightly address, Zelenskiy said Putin had given himself a birthday present by killing children, and that Russia wanted to strike painfully and demonstratively.

Chernihiv's regional governor, Viacheslav Chaus, said Putin belonged in The Hague or in hell. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan called Putin after the attack and offered to help arrange direct Russia–Ukraine talks. No Russian response was reported in the coverage reviewed.

With winter approaching, the damage to energy infrastructure adds to fears of a new campaign against Ukraine's power grid, as in previous years of the war.

Why it matters

The scale of the barrage and the civilian toll undercut talk of peace efforts and increase pressure on Ukraine's allies to supply more air defence before winter.
