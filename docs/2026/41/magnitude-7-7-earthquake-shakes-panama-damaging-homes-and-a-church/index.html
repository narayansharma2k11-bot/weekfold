Magnitude 7.7 earthquake shakes Panama, damaging homes and a church

A shallow quake struck central-southern Panama on Friday, sending thousands into the streets but causing no reported deaths in early accounts.

A magnitude 7.7 earthquake hit Panama on Friday, according to the US Geological Survey, which first put it at 8.0 and then revised it. The shallow quake, about 12.6 km deep, struck near Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province, about 200 km from Panama City. Buildings were damaged and some collapsed in Penonomé, where a church was also hit, and hundreds of people evacuated buildings in the capital. A tsunami alert was issued for the eastern Pacific.

No deaths or injuries were reported in early accounts, although the USGS warned casualties were possible. More than 40 aftershocks followed, including one of about magnitude 6. The Panama Canal and the Cobre Panamá copper mine reported normal operations, and Tocumen airport lifted a brief flight suspension.

Why it matters

Panama hosts one of the world's most important shipping routes, so a major quake there is closely watched by global trade.
