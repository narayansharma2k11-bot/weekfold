AfD lawmaker elected Saxony-Anhalt speaker, a first since 1945

Tobias Rausch has become the first far-right president of a German state parliament since the Nazi era.

On 6 October, Saxony-Anhalt's state parliament in Magdeburg elected Tobias Rausch, 35, of the Alternative for Germany (AfD), as its president on the first ballot. He received 48 votes, while 29 lawmakers voted against him and five abstained. His total included the five members of the Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance and several other lawmakers from outside the AfD. The CDU leadership had recommended abstaining, and the SPD, Greens and Left opposed him.

The AfD won 39 of 83 seats in last month's state election, three short of a majority. Mainstream parties still refuse to govern with it. The AfD's Ulrich Siegmund plans to run for minister-president in December, but he may not have the votes. Rausch has previously been at the centre of a nepotism controversy over staff hiring.

Why it matters

The vote shows the far right gaining institutional power in Germany despite the long-standing cordon around the AfD.
