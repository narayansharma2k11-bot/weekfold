Trump strikes deal with Putin to import Russian diesel

The US president says Russia will ship nearly 5 million tonnes of diesel to US and world markets, easing pressure on Moscow ahead of the midterms.

President Donald Trump said on Friday that after a call with Vladimir Putin, Russia would supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and then a further 4 million tonnes in stages. He said the aim was to bring down fuel costs for farmers and truckers; US diesel averaged $6.23 a gallon, close to September's record. The Treasury issued an unusually long general licence that keeps sanctions off such diesel cargoes until April 2027.

The US has not imported Russian oil or gas since 2022, and the deal makes it less likely that new tariffs on buyers of Russian energy will be enforced soon. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it a weak decision by strong partners. Russia, which had banned diesel exports since July after Ukrainian strikes on its refineries, said it would lift those curbs early.

Why it matters

The deal loosens Western economic pressure on Russia over the Ukraine war in exchange for relief on global fuel prices.
