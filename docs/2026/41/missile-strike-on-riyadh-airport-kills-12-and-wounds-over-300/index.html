Conflict · Saturday 10 October 2026

Missile strike on Riyadh airport kills 12 and wounds over 300

A projectile hit a passenger terminal at King Khalid International Airport, the deadliest attack on Saudi soil in more than a decade, as Yemen's war spreads.

A projectile struck Terminal 4 at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Saturday, starting a fire and killing at least 12 people, Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said. It said 309 people were wounded, some critically. The dead included four Saudis, two Bangladeshis and one person each from the US, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan and Egypt. India's embassy later said an injured Indian national had also died.

It was the third attack on the airport in a week. Strikes on Thursday killed three Saudis, including a Saudia pilot, and hit a parked airliner. Saudi officials described Saturday's blast as the deadliest attack on the kingdom in more than ten years. Flights were suspended, several airlines halted Riyadh services, and schools in the capital moved online for a week. The Houthi media office said the group had shut down the airport, and a separate attack was reported at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam.

The violence follows the collapse of the truce in Yemen. On 4 October Saudi-backed government forces launched an offensive to retake Houthi-held areas, backed by Saudi air strikes on Sanaa. The Houthis responded by declaring Saudi airspace a military zone and naming the kingdom's airports as targets.

The Saudi-led coalition called the strike a full-fledged war crime and promised a decisive response. The UN, the EU, Qatar, India, Pakistan and others condemned it. President Donald Trump said what happened was unacceptable and that the US was weighing whether to join strikes on the Houthis. The US, UK, Canada and others told citizens to avoid the airport.

Analysts expect strong Saudi retaliation, though Riyadh may still look for a way to de-escalate. The Houthis claimed another attack on the airport on Sunday, and there is no timeline for full reopening.

Why it matters

Attacks on a Gulf capital's main airport widen Yemen's war, threaten regional aviation and could draw the US deeper into the conflict linked to Iran.
