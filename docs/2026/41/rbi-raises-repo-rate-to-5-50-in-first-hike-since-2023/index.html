RBI raises repo rate to 5.50% in first hike since 2023

India's central bank lifted rates by 25 basis points and moved to a 'calibrated tightening' stance as inflation risks build.

On 7 October, the Reserve Bank of India's six-member Monetary Policy Committee voted unanimously to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%. It was the first increase since February 2023. Governor Sanjay Malhotra announced a shift in stance from neutral to calibrated tightening, which means no near-term rate cuts. Consumer inflation has risen for ten straight months to 4.8% in August, above the RBI's 4% target.

The bank raised its inflation forecast for the 2026–27 financial year to 5.2% and cut its growth forecast to 7.1%. A weak monsoon, crude oil near $100 a barrel, high US bond yields and a rupee at about 96 to the dollar all added to the pressure.

Why it matters

The hike marks a turn in policy for one of the world's fastest-growing major economies and shows how oil prices and global interest rates are squeezing emerging markets.
