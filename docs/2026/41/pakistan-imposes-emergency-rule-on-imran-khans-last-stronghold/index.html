Pakistan imposes emergency rule on Imran Khan's last stronghold

Islamabad suspended the PTI-run government of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and put the province under its governor, citing failures against militancy.

Pakistan declared a state of emergency and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Sunday, sidelining the provincial government run by jailed former prime minister Imran Khan's party, Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI). President Asif Ali Zardari approved recommendations from Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's cabinet, invoking Article 232 of the constitution on emergencies and Article 234 on the breakdown of constitutional machinery in a province.

The federal government said the provincial administration had deliberately disengaged from the fight against terrorism, and it has also criticised the province's stance on talks with the Afghan Taliban. Governor Faisal Karim Kundi now runs the province with an 11-member advisory council of technocrats, a new chief secretary has been appointed, and army troops have been deployed to support civilian authorities. Internet disruptions were reported in parts of Peshawar.

PTI says Chief Minister Sohail Afridi and other party figures were arrested. Police have not confirmed this, and a federal minister said only that some people had been detained. PTI spokesperson Zulfikar Bukhari called the move a coup disguised as an emergency, and party chairman Gohar Ali Khan said it would be challenged in the courts and in parliament.

Tensions had been building for weeks. PTI had planned a march on Islamabad to demand Khan's release, which was postponed twice, and the government had vowed to stop it from entering the capital. Khan has been in prison for more than three years but remains widely popular.

Under the constitution, governor's rule lapses after two months unless parliament approves it in a joint sitting, and it can last no more than six months in total. Legal challenges and street protests are expected.

Why it matters

Suspending an elected opposition government in a volatile border province deepens Pakistan's political crisis and could complicate its fight against militancy near Afghanistan.
