Kagan and Soai share chemistry Nobel for mirror-image molecule breakthrough

The French and Japanese chemists showed how reactions can be pushed to produce only one 'handed' form of a molecule.

Henri Kagan, 95, of France, and Kenso Soai, 76, of the Tokyo University of Science, won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering nonlinear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis. Many molecules come in two mirror-image forms, like left and right hands, yet living things use almost only one. In the 1980s, Kagan's work suggested reactions could be designed to favour one form. Soai later created the first reaction that efficiently makes only one mirror image, now known as the Soai reaction.

The work matters for drug manufacturing because the two forms of a medicine can act very differently in the body. Committee member Peter Somfai called it probably the coolest experiment in organic chemistry. Soai, who heard the news while shopping, called it one of the most exciting days of his life.

Why it matters

Making a single mirror-image form of a molecule is essential to producing safe, effective medicines, and the work also sheds light on how life's chemistry began.
