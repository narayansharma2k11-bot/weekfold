Canadian poet Anne Carson wins the Nobel Prize in Literature

The Swedish Academy honoured the 76-year-old classicist and poet for work that reinvents ancient texts in new literary forms.

Anne Carson, the Toronto-born poet, essayist and translator of ancient Greek, has won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature. The Swedish Academy praised her "bold and inventive oeuvre" and said her writing builds new forms by playing with the classical tradition. The award is worth 12 million Swedish crowns, about $1.2 million.

Carson, 76, who has also held Icelandic citizenship since 2022, is best known for the verse novel Autobiography of Red, the narrative poem The Beauty of the Husband and the essay Eros the Bittersweet. The Academy's permanent secretary, Ingrid Carlberg, said Carson was caught by surprise and was in Iceland when reached. She follows Hungary's László Krasznahorkai, last year's laureate.

Why it matters

The prize brings global attention to genre-blending poetry and classical translation, and is a rare literature Nobel for a Canadian writer.
