Oil jumps toward $106 as Hormuz tanker attacks hit record

Brent crude climbed nearly 5% to around $105 a barrel as Iranian attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz intensified.

Oil prices surged on Thursday, with Brent rising almost 5% to about $105 a barrel and US crude gaining a similar margin to roughly $92.50. Traders reacted to a record run of tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz: Kpler counted ten tankers struck between 28 September and 4 October, well above the previous weekly high of six. On Tuesday only seven tankers crossed the strait, the fewest since late July.

An approaching hurricane in the US Gulf of Mexico added to supply worries as producers began shutting in output. The International Energy Agency said about 325 million barrels of emergency stocks had already been released, with around 100 million more to come. US diesel averaged $6.28 a gallon, according to AAA.

Why it matters

Hormuz carries a large share of the world's seaborne oil, so attacks there feed straight into global fuel prices and inflation.
