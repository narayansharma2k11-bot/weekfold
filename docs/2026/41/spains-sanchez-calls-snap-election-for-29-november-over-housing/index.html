Spain's Sánchez calls snap election for 29 November over housing

After parliament blocked his housing decrees, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez brought the general election forward to 29 November.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced on 5 October that Spain will vote on 29 November, about a year early. The previous week, right-wing opposition parties had combined to defeat two emergency decrees aimed at cutting evictions and property speculation. Housing has become a national flashpoint. The government said more than 70,000 people marched in Madrid, and anger grew after the televised eviction of an 87-year-old woman.

Sánchez has led a minority coalition with Sumar since 2023 and depends on smaller parties to pass laws. He is also under pressure from legal cases involving his party, his wife and his brother, all of whom deny wrongdoing. Polls point to a People's Party government backed by the far-right Vox. PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo framed the vote as a chance to remove Sánchez.

Why it matters

A PP–Vox win would bring a far-right party into Spain's national government for the first time since Franco.
