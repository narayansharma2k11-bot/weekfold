US freezes green-card sponsorship for eight tech and IT giants

The Labor Department has stopped accepting or processing permanent labour certifications from Microsoft, Adobe and six major IT outsourcing firms, citing alleged abuse of immigration rules.

The Trump administration on Thursday suspended eight large employers from the PERM programme, the labour-certification step that sits at the start of most employer-sponsored US green card cases. The companies named are Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, Cognizant, Capgemini, Microsoft and Adobe. Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said his department would neither accept new applications from them nor process ones already pending.

Officials gave different reasons for different firms. Sonderling said Microsoft and Adobe face several active federal investigations, while the six IT services companies were singled out largely for volume: together the eight have obtained more than 100,000 permanent labour certifications since 2009, he said. Vice President JD Vance accused Microsoft of cutting about 6,000 American jobs last year while securing roughly 6,300 H-1B visas and nearly 3,000 green cards. These are allegations, not established findings.

The move does not cancel H-1B visas or green cards already granted, and other employers are unaffected. But it could stall permanent residency for thousands of foreign tech workers, many of them Indian nationals on H-1B visas. No end date or path to reinstatement has been announced, and it is unclear how cases at later stages will be treated. India's foreign ministry has raised concerns about the effect on talent mobility.

The suspension is part of a broader immigration crackdown. Vance also said nine universities, including Harvard, Yale and Stanford, are under investigation by the Labor Department's inspector general over alleged misuse of student and exchange-visitor programmes, with subpoenas issued. A day earlier the administration proposed a $70,000 fee on universities that recommend foreign students for OPT work permits, adding to a $100,000 H-1B fee now being challenged in court.

Immigration lawyers expect tougher scrutiny of PERM filings across the board. The named companies had not publicly responded in the reports reviewed, and the length of the freeze remains the key unknown.

Why it matters

The freeze hits the main pipeline that moves skilled foreign workers, especially from India, into permanent US residency, and signals rising US pressure on global tech and outsourcing firms.
