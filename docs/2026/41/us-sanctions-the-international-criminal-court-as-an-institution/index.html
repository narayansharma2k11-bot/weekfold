US sanctions the International Criminal Court as an institution

Washington blocked the court's US assets and barred anyone from providing it funds, goods or services, sharply escalating its campaign against the tribunal.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced on Friday that the US was sanctioning the International Criminal Court itself, going beyond earlier measures against individual judges and prosecutors. The order freezes any court property in the US and bars contributions, goods or services to the court, which exposes companies that work with it to penalties. Temporary exemptions cover maintenance, technology and pension services. Rubio cited the court's Afghanistan investigation and its arrest warrants for Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant.

ICC Deputy Prosecutor Nazhat Shameem Khan said the sanctions should be condemned and the court's work would go on, and the court urged member states to step up support. Netanyahu welcomed the move. The court has reportedly prepared to replace some US-made software with European alternatives. The announcement came hours after former ICC judge Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize.

Why it matters

Sanctioning the court as a whole could disrupt its daily operations and weaken the main global body for prosecuting war crimes.
