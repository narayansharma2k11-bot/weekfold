Flávio Bolsonaro edges Lula as Brazil heads to 25 October runoff

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro finished narrowly ahead of President Lula in the first round, upsetting polls and setting up a runoff.

Brazil's presidential race goes to a second round on 25 October after neither leading candidate won a majority on 4 October. Flávio Bolsonaro, a 45-year-old senator, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro and an ally of Donald Trump, took about 47% of valid votes, or just over 56 million. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, who is seeking a fourth term, took about 45%, or 53.7 million. Pre-election polls had put Lula in front.

Lula's approval has fallen amid an affordability crisis and a corruption scandal. Bolsonaro is campaigning on the cost of living, a crackdown on crime and closer trade ties with Washington. Several eliminated candidates, including Augusto Cury, have declined to endorse either man. The winner takes office on 5 January.

Why it matters

The runoff will decide whether Latin America's largest economy moves closer to the US or keeps its distance and deepens ties with China.
