Myanmar jet bombs busy Rakhine market, killing dozens of civilians

A military air strike near the main wholesale market in Kyauktaw, in rebel-held Rakhine state, killed at least 33 people, a toll that later rose to 49.

A Myanmar military fighter jet dropped two bombs near the market in Kyauktaw, a town in northern Rakhine state, at around midday on Monday 28 September, according to the Arakan Army, the ethnic armed group that controls the area. Vendors, shoppers and passers-by were among the victims. The first count put the dead at 33 with 36 wounded, and the rebels warned the figure would climb.

By early Tuesday the Arakan Army and a senior local rescue official, Wai Hun Aung, said at least 49 people had died and 58 were injured, 22 of them critically. The market is the region's main wholesale hub, drawing traders from across the state. Images shared online showed burning buildings, wrecked vehicles and a collapsed section of a nearby bridge. The casualty figures could not be independently verified, and the military did not immediately respond; it has long said it strikes only legitimate targets and describes resistance groups as terrorists. Wai Hun Aung called it "one of the most severe airstrikes in Rakhine."

The attack fits a pattern of deadly air raids in areas the army has lost. Since launching an offensive in November 2023, the Arakan Army has taken a key regional military headquarters and 14 of Rakhine's 17 townships. The military has choked trade routes into the state, worsening food shortages, and in December an air strike on a hospital in Mrauk-U township killed more than 30 people.

Myanmar has been at war with itself since the army ousted Aung San Suu Kyi's elected government in February 2021. Former junta chief Min Aung Hlaing was installed as president by a military-backed parliament in April. The UN says nearly half a million people are displaced in Rakhine, and the ACLED monitoring project estimates that more than 100,000 people have been killed nationwide.

The Arakan Army promised swift retaliation, raising the prospect of further fighting in the west of the country. Rights groups have repeatedly called for an arms and aviation-fuel embargo to curb the military's air campaign.

Why it matters

Air strikes on civilian markets are among the deadliest features of Myanmar's civil war, which has displaced millions and destabilised a region bordering Bangladesh, India and China.
