Swiss glaciers lose more than 5% of their ice in a year

Monitors say 2026 was the second-worst melt year on record, with several small glaciers vanishing entirely.

Switzerland's glaciers lost about 5.5% of their remaining ice in 2026, according to the annual report from the GLAMOS monitoring network and the Swiss Academy of Sciences. That is just short of the record 6% loss in 2022. Allalin, Clariden, Aletsch and Rhône glaciers had their greatest melt ever, and small ice fields such as Bella Tola disappeared. Swiss ice volume has fallen by almost a fifth in five years.

A dry winter left little protective snow, and the freezing level sat above 4,000 metres on a record 76 days between May and September. Glaciologist Matthias Huss described one site as "a moon landscape that is dry and dead." Low river levels this summer disrupted Rhine and Danube shipping, and scientists project Swiss glaciers could vanish by 2100 on current warming.

Why it matters

Alpine glaciers act as Europe's water towers, feeding rivers used for drinking water, farming, shipping and power-plant cooling.
