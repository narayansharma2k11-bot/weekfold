Hurricane Polo slams into Mexico's Baja California peninsula

Polo made landfall late on Monday in Baja California Sur before crossing the Gulf of California to hit Sonora and soak the US Southwest.

Hurricane Polo came ashore in Baja California Sur late on 28 September local time, near the middle of the peninsula in Comondú municipality, with sustained winds of around 110 mph (177 km/h). Hundreds of people took shelter, ports closed and the Mexican navy sent ships to help isolated coastal villages. The state governor reported mainly rain damage and no deaths or injuries.

The storm then crossed the Gulf of California and struck the Sonora coast near Guaymas on Tuesday as a weaker hurricane, flooding streets and knocking out power. Its remnants brought flash floods to Arizona, Utah and New Mexico, where some riverside communities were told to move to higher ground over fears of a dam failure, and flood watches stretched into the central US.

Why it matters

Polo was one of several strong storms in an active Eastern Pacific season, and its moisture caused flooding hundreds of miles inland in the United States.
