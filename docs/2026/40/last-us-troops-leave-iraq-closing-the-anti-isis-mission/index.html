Last US troops leave Iraq, closing the anti-ISIS mission

American and coalition forces completed their departure from Erbil Air Base, ending Operation Inherent Resolve in Iraq after 12 years.

US Central Command said on Wednesday 30 September that the last American troops and equipment had left Erbil Air Base in Iraq's Kurdistan region, completing the withdrawal of US and coalition forces from the country. The move ends Operation Inherent Resolve in Iraq, the campaign against the Islamic State group that began in 2014, under a transition plan Baghdad and Washington agreed in September 2024.

Erbil had been the main hub for the coalition task force and hosted most of the roughly 1,500 American and allied personnel still supporting counter-ISIS work. Most US forces left bases in federal Iraq last year. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell described an orderly departure, and CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said ISIS "no longer poses a systemic threat" to Iraq's security. President Trump called it a decisive victory.

US forces invaded Iraq in 2003, withdrew in 2011 and were invited back in 2014 after ISIS overran large parts of Iraq and Syria. Iraq declared territorial victory in 2017, but the coalition stayed to prevent a comeback. The task force has now moved its headquarters to Jordan and will keep fighting ISIS in Syria alongside partners and the Syrian government.

Reaction in Iraq was mixed. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi spoke of a new phase built on sovereignty, and an official of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia welcomed the exit. The Kurdish region's Peshmerga ministry, however, said leaving without providing an air-defence system was worrying, noting the region had faced more than 1,000 drone and missile attacks during the recent US-Iran conflict.

Relations will now shift to a conventional bilateral defence partnership, with Washington offering training and intelligence. Iraqi forces take primary responsibility for remaining ISIS cells and for the Iran-backed militias that analysts expect to test the new arrangement.

Why it matters

The exit ends more than two decades of near-continuous US military involvement in Iraq at a time when the wider region is gripped by the US-Iran war.
