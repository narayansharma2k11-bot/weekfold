Merz brings €1.3 billion aid package to Kyiv amid bridge strikes

Germany's chancellor pledged military and energy aid as Russian drones hit another Kyiv bridge during his visit.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz made a visit to Kyiv on 4 October and announced more than €1.3 billion in new support: about €1 billion in military aid, including long-range weapons, combat drones and satellite technology, and €350 million to repair the energy grid before winter. German firms signed defence deals with Ukraine covering IRIS-T air-defence missiles, radar and jet-drone interceptors; Reuters put 14 deals at about €6.6 billion, while Zelensky cited 15 agreements worth more than €8.5 billion.

During the visit, a Russian drone struck Kyiv's Northern Bridge, wounding two people; three of the city's six road bridges have been fully or partly closed since Thursday. Merz said Germany "will not be intimidated" and urged Vladimir Putin to end the war. The US has proposed technical-level three-way talks by late October.

Why it matters

Germany is Ukraine's biggest European backer, and its aid is central to Kyiv's ability to defend cities and power plants from Russia's escalating drone war.
