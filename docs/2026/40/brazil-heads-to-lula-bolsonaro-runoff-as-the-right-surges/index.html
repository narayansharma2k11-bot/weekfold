Brazil heads to Lula-Bolsonaro runoff as the right surges

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro took about 47% to President Lula's 45% in the first round, setting up a 25 October runoff.

Brazil's presidential election will be decided in a runoff on 25 October after neither candidate passed 50% of valid votes on Sunday 4 October. With nearly all ballots counted, right-wing senator Flávio Bolsonaro had about 47%, roughly 56 million votes, against about 45% for left-wing President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Most pre-election polls had shown Lula finishing first, so the result surprised analysts.

Bolsonaro, 45, is the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who lost narrowly to Lula in 2022 and is now serving a 27-year sentence under house arrest for plotting to overturn that result. Flávio has promised to continue his father's legacy, cut the deficit and realign foreign policy with Washington. He declared the vote "the end of the Workers' Party era." Lula, 80 and seeking a fourth term, said a new campaign begins now and that he thrives in knockout rounds.

The right also did well in Congress. Bolsonaro's Liberal Party was on course to become the largest party in the lower house and gained Senate seats; Michelle Bolsonaro won a Senate seat in the Federal District, and Carlos Bolsonaro was elected senator in Santa Catarina. Third- to fifth-placed presidential candidates were all on the right, although two of them, Ronaldo Caiado and Renan Santos, said they would not endorse anyone.

The United States looms over the race. Donald Trump's administration is pushing to shift Latin America rightward; Lula has accused Washington of meddling, which the State Department denies, and has campaigned on protecting Brazil's sovereignty. Voters say security, corruption and the economy are their top concerns.

Lula must now rebuild the broad coalition that elected him in 2022, including less enthusiastic young voters, in a three-week sprint. Observers will also watch for a repeat of the political violence that marked the last runoff.

Why it matters

Brazil is Latin America's largest economy and democracy, and the runoff will decide whether it stays on a left-leaning, independent course or aligns with Trump's Washington.
