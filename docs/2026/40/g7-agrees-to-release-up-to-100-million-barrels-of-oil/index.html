G7 agrees to release up to 100 million barrels of oil

Leaders backed a four-month release of emergency reserves, front-loaded with diesel, to calm record fuel prices.

G7 countries agreed on 2 October to release up to 100 million barrels of strategic oil reserves over four months, coordinated through the International Energy Agency, after a call chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron. The plan includes a large diesel release within the first 20 days, coordinated refinery maintenance and a pledge to avoid energy export restrictions between G7 members.

Diesel prices have hit records on both sides of the Atlantic: the UK average passed £2 a litre for the first time, and the US average reached $6.37 a gallon. Donald Trump, who had floated banning US diesel exports unless Europe released its stocks, claimed credit for the deal. The IEA's 32 members had already agreed a 400-million-barrel release in March.

Why it matters

Coordinated stock releases are the main tool rich countries have to cushion households from the oil shock caused by the Gulf war.
