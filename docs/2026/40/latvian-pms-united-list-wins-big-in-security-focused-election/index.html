Latvian PM's United List wins big in security-focused election

Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs' centre-right party took about 35% of the vote, while a party critics call pro-Russian came third.

Latvians voted on 3 October in a parliamentary election dominated by fears of Russia, living costs and strained health care. Preliminary results showed Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs' United List with about 35% and a projected 42 of the 100 seats, its best result since independence. Latvia First, led by a populist property magnate, came second, and Sovereign Power, which opposes military aid to Ukraine, came third. Turnout was about 52%.

Kulbergs, in office since May, said voters wanted change, but one of his coalition partners, the Union of Greens and Farmers, fell just short of the 5% threshold, so he must find new allies. Most mainstream parties have ruled out working with Sovereign Power. Official results are due by 19 October.

Why it matters

Latvia sits on NATO's eastern flank, and its politics on defence and Ukraine are closely watched as Russia steps up pressure on Europe.
