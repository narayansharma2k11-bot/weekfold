US hiring nearly stalls with just 29,000 jobs in September

Payroll growth missed forecasts, earlier months were revised down by 60,000 and unemployment rose to 4.2%.

US employers added only 29,000 jobs in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday 2 October, well below the roughly 90,000 economists expected and under the 45,000 monthly average of the past year. The unemployment rate edged up to 4.2% from 4.1%, leaving 7.1 million people out of work. The rate has stayed between 4.1% and 4.3% since March.

Revisions made the picture weaker. July's figure was cut from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000, and August's from 162,000 to 133,000, removing 60,000 jobs in total. Health care added 17,000 jobs, construction 11,000 and manufacturing 9,000, while government shed 17,000, information 10,000 and financial activities 7,000.

Pay growth slowed. Average hourly earnings rose just 5 cents to $37.81, up 3.0% from a year earlier, which analysts at First Trust noted is lagging consumer price inflation. The household survey was firmer: participation rose to 61.8% and the labour force grew by nearly half a million people, suggesting the job market is cooling rather than collapsing.

The report lands as the US economy absorbs high fuel prices driven by the war with Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and days after consumer confidence fell to its lowest since 2014. Federal government jobs have dropped sharply over the past year, while factory and construction hiring has been supported by data-centre building.

For the Federal Reserve, which raised rates in September, the soft numbers make another hike at its October meeting less likely, according to First Trust, pushing the debate towards December. The next jobs report is due in early November, just after the midterm elections.

Why it matters

The US labour market underpins global demand, and a stalling job engine could tip the world's largest economy towards recession while inflation stays high.
