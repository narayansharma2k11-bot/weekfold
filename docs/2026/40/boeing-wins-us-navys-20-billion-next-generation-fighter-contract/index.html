Boeing wins US Navy's $20 billion next-generation fighter contract

The F/A-XX award gives Boeing both of America's sixth-generation fighter programmes.

The US Navy awarded Boeing a development contract worth about $20 billion for its F/A-XX carrier-based sixth-generation fighter, announced late on 29 September. Northrop Grumman had competed for the work. The aircraft will eventually replace the F/A-18 Super Hornet and EA-18G Growler and fly alongside F-35Cs and combat drones. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao called it a generational leap in air superiority.

Boeing already won the Air Force's F-47 programme in March 2025, and will build both jets mainly in St. Louis. Congress revived F/A-XX after the Pentagon had tried to slow it to prioritise the F-47. Boeing shares rose about 3% on the news, and some analysts suggest the new jet could fly before the end of the decade.

Why it matters

The contract shapes how the US Navy will project power from aircraft carriers for decades and reshapes the competitive landscape of the global defence industry.
