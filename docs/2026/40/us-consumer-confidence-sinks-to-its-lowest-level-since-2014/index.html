US consumer confidence sinks to its lowest level since 2014

The Conference Board's index fell to 81.9 in September as fuel prices and job worries weighed on households.

The Conference Board said on 29 September that its US consumer confidence index fell 6.7 points to 81.9, the weakest reading in about 12 years and far below the roughly 89 economists had expected. Its gauge of current conditions dropped to 109.3, and households' view of present business conditions turned negative for the first time since September 2024.

The expectations index slid to 63.6, its third straight fall and the 20th month in a row below 80, a level the group associates with recession risk. Written comments about prices, especially for oil and petrol, reached new highs, and more consumers expected interest rates to rise over the next year. One economist called the data a warning sign for holiday spending.

Why it matters

US consumers drive about two-thirds of the world's largest economy, so a slump in their mood can ripple through global trade and markets.
