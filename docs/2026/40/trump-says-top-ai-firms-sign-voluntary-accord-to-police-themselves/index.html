Trump says top AI firms sign voluntary accord to police themselves

Leaders of Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, Nvidia and xAI agreed at the White House to internal controls and outside audits.

After meeting AI executives at the White House on 29 September, President Trump announced a voluntary accord signed by the heads of Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia and xAI and by OpenAI's president. The companies pledged to set up robust internal controls, hire independent auditors to test them, and create board committees to review the results, with a note that the steps might one day become law. Trump called the pact "morally binding" and said a small committee would oversee it.

Critics, including academics at USC and Harvard's Berkman Klein Center, said self-policing and vague wording are no substitute for legal liability and regulation. The announcement came a day after OpenAI said it was holding back a new model after internal safety tests, and on the same day it launched 'dots', an always-on personal AI agent.

Why it matters

With Washington refusing binding AI rules, voluntary pledges by a handful of US firms are effectively setting the guardrails for a technology used worldwide.
