Israel's election ends with no clear winner as Arab parties surge

Netanyahu's bloc led but fell short of a majority, with record turnout and an unprecedented showing for Arab parties.

Israelis voted on Tuesday in their first general election since the 7 October 2023 attacks and the war in Gaza, and the result left neither side with a majority. With more than 85 percent of ballots counted, AFP reported that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc was on track for 53 of the 120 Knesset seats, former army chief Gadi Eisenkot's opposition alliance for 48, and Arab parties for 19. A coalition needs 61. Al Jazeera, working from an earlier count, put the bloc at 54 seats and reported that turnout of 77.7 percent was the highest since 1999.

Both camps claimed momentum. Eisenkot said the result would be "a new start", while Netanyahu said Likud was the largest party and called for a national right-wing government. Likud later accused the Arab lists of having stolen the election, alleging corruption and threats, but gave no evidence. Netanyahu has also called the idea of relying on Arab parties a disgrace.

The Arab parties are now kingmakers in all but name. Yair Golan, a leading opposition figure, called for an alliance with Arab lawmakers, while Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen said anyone seeking a real alternative to Netanyahu would have to talk to his party, though he told AFP it would not join a coalition outright. Eisenkot has ruled out serving with Netanyahu.

President Isaac Herzog urged parties to do everything to avoid another election, and he is reportedly backing a unity government. He will consult party leaders and name a candidate, who then has up to 42 days to assemble a coalition. If that fails, Israel heads back to the polls. Netanyahu stays on as caretaker prime minister in the meantime, and Al Jazeera said official results are due on 4 November.

The vote also unfolds against the war with Iran. AFP noted that Netanyahu ordered two campaigns against Iran, and that Trump has said Washington will not attack again until after the US midterms.

Why it matters

A hung Knesset means weeks of coalition bargaining, possible new elections, and a government whose makeup will shape Israel's Gaza and Iran policy.
