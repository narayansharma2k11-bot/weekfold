Binance says $6.3 billion stolen in the biggest crypto heist on record

Analysts blame a North Korean unit; about $3 billion was frozen and the exchange says it will cover users' losses.

Binance said about $6.3 billion in crypto was stolen from the exchange, which appears to be the largest crypto theft ever. Al Jazeera noted the previous record was the roughly $1.5 billion taken from Bybit in February 2025. TRM Labs and Chainalysis say most of the theft occurred over about 20 minutes starting late Thursday. The exchange suspended deposits and withdrawals, and founder Changpeng Zhao said on X that it had likely been hacked, TechCrunch reported.

Reuters, cited by Al Jazeera, said about $3 billion has been frozen, including more than $2.3 billion by stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle; Binance has not said where the other $3.3 billion went. TRM said with high confidence that TraderTraitor, a North Korean unit linked to the Lazarus Group, carried out the attack, and Chainalysis reached the same conclusion. North Korea routinely denies such claims. Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said the investigation had not attributed responsibility to any individual.

Binance says user funds are safe and that it will cover all losses. It first said the breach might have started with a hacked engineer's laptop, then cited additional points of breach, and said it found no evidence that a wallet private key leaked. It said it is on track to resume withdrawals within 45 hours. Bitcoin touched a six-week low of about $76,368, and Coinbase, which said it was unaffected, fell 4.8 percent.

Why it matters

A theft of this size, attributed to a state-linked hacking unit, tests how well exchanges protect customer money and how fast stablecoin issuers can freeze stolen funds.
