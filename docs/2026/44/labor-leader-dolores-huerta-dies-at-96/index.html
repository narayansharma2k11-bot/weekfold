Labor leader Dolores Huerta dies at 96

The co-founder of the United Farm Workers, who coined "Sí se puede", died on Wednesday.

Dolores Huerta, the farmworker organiser and civil rights icon, died on Wednesday at the age of 96, Democracy Now! reported. Born in 1930, she co-founded the National Farm Workers Association in 1962 with Cesar Chavez; it later merged with the Filipino-led Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee to form the United Farm Workers of America. The union organised the Delano grape strike from 1965 to 1970, which ended in major gains for farmworkers' labor rights.

Huerta coined the slogan "¡Sí se puede!", which she has said she came up with in Arizona in 1972 during Chavez's 25-day water-only fast. She also worked in the women's movement and the immigrant rights movement and was a prominent supporter of the Democratic Party.

Her legacy took on a painful new dimension this year. After a New York Times investigation in March into Chavez's sexual abuse of women and girls in the farmworkers' movement, Huerta said Chavez had raped her in the 1960s and that the assaults led to the births of two of her children, whom she had kept secret. Journalist Maria Hinojosa, who interviewed her afterwards, described her as a perennial optimist who loved life and said Huerta told her she had been crying all morning when the story came out.

Why it matters

Huerta's life tied the farmworker movement to the wider fights for women's and immigrant rights, and her final public chapter forced a reckoning with the movement's own history.
