Trump closes midterm campaign as record early voting tops 52 million

With Election Day on Tuesday, Republicans are defending a Senate majority, and courts are ruling on fights over ballots and voter checks.

President Donald Trump is wrapping up a turbulent midterm campaign with rallies in two states he has carried before: Iowa on Sunday afternoon, where Republicans are trying to hold a Senate seat and the governor's office, and Kansas on Tuesday, Election Day, according to the Associated Press. Trump cancelled and then abruptly rescheduled the stops as Republicans work to protect a Senate majority the AP described as at risk of slipping away. More than 52 million votes have already been cast.

AP reported that the elections are becoming a referendum on Trump's second term, with voters, including past supporters, questioning the economic fallout of his tariffs and of the war in Iran. In Kansas, Democrat Adam Hamilton is running a strong bid to become the state's first Democratic senator in 94 years. Democrats face their own problems in Michigan, where Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed is feuding publicly with Rep. Haley Stevens. Senator Tim Scott, who chairs the Republican Senate campaign committee, said on NBC that Trump's agenda is on the ballot.

The legal fights are heating up. Just Security's roundup noted that a federal judge, David Carter, rejected the Justice Department's bid to force California to set aside 20,000 ballots from people it called likely noncitizens, saying it would disrupt an election already underway; the administration had also asked Washington state to set aside hundreds of ballots. A civil-rights group sued over a plan to send hundreds of investigators to find noncitizen voters.

In Alaska, the governor ordered a criminal investigation after the New York Times reported that Democratic Senate nominee Mary Peltola's campaign and party operatives recruited a second candidate named Dan Sullivan to confuse voters. Peltola and the Democratic Senate campaign committee deny the report, though a former campaign manager told the Times that Peltola knew of the plan.

Election Day is Tuesday 3 November.

Why it matters

Control of Congress decides whether Trump's second-term agenda, and the next phase of the Iran war, faces checks.
