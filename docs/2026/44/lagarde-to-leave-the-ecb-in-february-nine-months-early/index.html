Lagarde to leave the ECB in February, nine months early

The European Central Bank president will step down on 7 February 2027, giving EU leaders time to pick a successor before French elections.

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde announced on Thursday that she will step down on 7 February 2027, nine months before her eight-year term was due to end on 31 October 2027. The 70-year-old said in a short video that she leaves with a sense of good work done and an ECB fit for the future, according to Reuters and the Associated Press.

The timing is political. Her early exit lets EU leaders choose a successor before the French presidential election in spring 2027, which polls suggest Marine Le Pen is favoured to win. EU Council President Antonio Costa said the succession process would start immediately. Reuters named Dutch former central bank head Klaas Knot and Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos as frontrunners, and noted that Isabel Schnabel leaves the ECB's board in early January and Philip Lane's term ends in May, so the posts may be filled as a package. Berenberg's Holger Schmieding said leaders would likely aim to settle them before a summit on 17-18 December.

Markets barely reacted. The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.5 percent this week, signalling another rise could come in December, while bond yields, especially in France, are climbing. Jean-Philippe Tanguy, a senior Le Pen economic aide, said he saw it coming, while German MEP Markus Ferber said it sent the wrong signal.

Why it matters

Choosing the next head of the euro zone's central bank during a rate-hiking cycle and a French election run-up makes this one of Europe's most consequential appointments.
