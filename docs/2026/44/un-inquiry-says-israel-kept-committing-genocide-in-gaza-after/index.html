UN inquiry says Israel kept committing genocide in Gaza after ceasefire

A UN-mandated commission says Israeli forces continued genocidal acts after the October 2025 truce; Israel calls the report a misinformation campaign.

A UN-mandated Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory said on Monday that Israel kept committing genocide in Gaza even after the US-brokered ceasefire took hold in October 2025. In a report covering violations from 7 October 2023 to September 2026, the commission said Israel was committing "genocidal acts with the genocidal intent to destroy" Palestinians in Gaza, according to Reuters. The panel also accused Israel of crimes against humanity in the occupied West Bank.

The report describes a ceasefire that did not stop the violence. Israeli forces kept using heavy munitions, drones, artillery and sniper fire in densely populated areas, Reuters reported, while the so-called Yellow Line moved deeper into Gaza and hardened into a de facto border. The Associated Press said Israel's control of the territory grew from 53 percent to at least 60 percent since the truce, and that Israeli fire killed at least 196 Palestinians in that period, including 18 women, 32 boys and 11 girls. Palestinian authorities say more than 74,000 people have been killed in Gaza since 7 October 2023.

On the West Bank, the commission said Israeli soldiers and prison guards committed war crimes and crimes against humanity, including extermination, torture, rape and persecution. It counted 254 deaths from airstrikes between 7 October 2023 and 30 September 2026, against six in the 15 years before the war, and said displacement since January 2025 is the largest there since 1967, with 34,176 Palestinians still displaced as of August. AP reported six recent cases of Israeli guards sexually abusing male detainees from Gaza at the Sde Teiman military prison, and noted it could not independently corroborate several of the graphic accounts. The commission also faulted the Palestinian Authority for targeting dissenting Palestinians.

Israel's mission in Geneva rejected the findings, calling the report part of a misinformation campaign to demonise the country and calling for the commission to be dissolved. Israeli ambassador Daniel Meron said such efforts undermine Israel's legitimacy, and the mission urged the international community to repudiate the report. Israel has long denied the genocide and sexual-assault allegations. The commission's chair, Srinivasan Muralidhar, said the killings and home demolitions in the West Bank were part of a wider strategy to displace Palestinians.

The report now goes to the UN General Assembly. AP noted that its findings could be used by International Criminal Court prosecutors or in other jurisdictions seeking accountability. The commission's previous report, in September 2025, also concluded that genocide was taking place and said Israeli leaders had incited it, an accusation Israel called scandalous.

Why it matters

The finding that a ceasefire has not ended alleged genocidal conduct raises the legal and diplomatic pressure on Israel and its allies, and it feeds directly into possible court cases.
