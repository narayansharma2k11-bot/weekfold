Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 confirmed cases

The Bundibugyo outbreak is now Congo's largest ever and the second-largest on record, with cases reported in Kenya and Nigeria.

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo rose above 10,000 on Tuesday, according to government data cited by Ghanamma. Congo's public health institute put the toll at 10,060 cases and 4,887 deaths. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo species and announced in mid-May, is now the largest and deadliest in Congo's history, surpassing the 2018-2020 epidemic.

It is also the second-largest and second-deadliest Ebola outbreak on record, behind the 2014-2016 West African epidemic, which infected more than 28,600 people and killed more than 11,000. Kenya reported its first case earlier this month and has announced nine in total, including a man who travelled from Congo via Uganda and died in Nairobi. Last week Nigeria became the fourth African country to report a case. A Swiss diplomat's wife who had not travelled to Congo was flown home on Monday, and Switzerland believes she was infected in Uganda. Uganda says it has no cases now and will quarantine returning citizens for 21 days.

Democracy Now! reported that youths armed with machetes attacked two community health workers who were raising Ebola awareness in Congo, killing one and wounding the other. A UN spokesperson said such incidents show how violence and mistrust can undermine the response.

Why it matters

Spread beyond Congo and attacks on health workers suggest the outbreak is outpacing containment efforts.
