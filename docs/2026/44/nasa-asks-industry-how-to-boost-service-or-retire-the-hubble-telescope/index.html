NASA asks industry how to boost, service or retire the Hubble telescope

A request for information issued Thursday seeks commercial options to extend Hubble's life before its orbit decays.

NASA issued a request for information on 29 October asking companies how they could service the Hubble Space Telescope, which has no propulsion and is slowly sinking as atmospheric drag lowers its orbit. The request is not a contract award. It covers three options: a mission to boost Hubble to a higher altitude, servicing to enhance its capabilities, or a controlled re-entry or disposal at the end of its life. Responses are due at 3 p.m. EST on 30 November.

NASA's analysis predicts Hubble would re-enter around 2033 to 2034 if left alone, and the agency expects any rescue mission would need to fly by 2032. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said extending Hubble's life would mean more discoveries and more science, and Nicky Fox, head of the Science Mission Directorate, said the agency is eager to explore the possibilities. Fox also referred to a flagship mission to follow Hubble.

Hubble, launched in 1990, was last repaired by astronauts in 2009 on the fifth shuttle servicing mission, which also installed a device meant to help a future spacecraft dock. Tech Times noted that NASA declined a private reboost proposal from Isaacman in 2024, before he became administrator.

Why it matters

Hubble is still producing science, and the request is the first sign NASA may accept outside help to keep it flying rather than let it fall.
