Pentagon says Iran war has cost $47 billion as talks edge forward

The tally is up more than $3 billion in a month, while Vance and Gulf mediators try to revive negotiations before an expected US escalation after the midterms.

The Pentagon has told Congress that the US war on Iran has cost at least $47 billion, up more than $3 billion since last month, according to a US Central Command estimate reported by Democracy Now! and Al Jazeera. About $29 billion of the total is for replacing munitions. Foreign Exchanges, a daily newsletter, called the figure an underestimate. The administration is reportedly prepared to resume heavy attacks after next week's US midterm elections.

Diplomacy is moving in parallel. The Wall Street Journal reported that Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, to try to restart peace talks, at Trump's direction. Axios reported that Qatar-led mediators are working on a deal under which Iran would let UN inspectors into bombed nuclear sites in return for some sanctions relief. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said he was in close contact with mediators on a proposed seven-day plan to pave the way for reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

At sea, Iranian officials said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines while trying to cross the strait, and the Revolutionary Guards claimed four vessels were hit; neither claim was independently confirmed, and Central Command insists it has cleared the waterway. Democracy Now! said a tanker struck two weeks ago has left an oil slick more than 20 miles long and a spill has fouled beaches in northern parts of the United Arab Emirates.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper briefed Trump in person at the White House on military options. Just Security noted that US and Israeli officials say renewed bombing is likely soon after the midterms unless a last-minute deal emerges, and that South Korea is weighing options short of combat for a Hormuz deployment.

Also on Thursday, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth fired Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Joint Staff's strategy and policy chief, without explanation, The Atlantic reported. Axios, cited by Foreign Exchanges, said Sylvia had reportedly admitted leaking classified information on Ukraine and Iran after failing polygraphs.

Why it matters

The bill and the timetable show the war is on hold rather than over, with a decision on escalation tied to the US election calendar.
