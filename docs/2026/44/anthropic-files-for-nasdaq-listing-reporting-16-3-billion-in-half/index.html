Anthropic files for Nasdaq listing, reporting $16.3 billion in half-year revenue

The AI company filed its S-1 on Monday for a listing under the ticker ANT, disclosing fast growth, huge compute commitments and a large non-cash loss.

Anthropic filed a Form S-1 with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, setting up an initial public offering of Class A shares on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The price range and share count were left blank. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a group of about 22 banks, according to The Next Web and IPOScoop.

The filing says revenue for the first half of 2026 was $16.3 billion, up from $1.16 billion a year earlier. Second-quarter revenue was $11.6 billion, with a first operating profit of $974 million, though the half-year operating result was a $693 million loss. The net loss for the half was $80.7 billion, mostly a $72.4 billion non-cash charge from revaluing convertible notes. More than 11,900 customers pay at least $100,000 a year.

Timing and size are still reports rather than facts. The Wall Street Journal, cited by The Next Web, said Anthropic aims to list the week of 16 November at a valuation of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion; Bloomberg, cited by IPOScoop, said marketing could begin the week of 9 November; Reuters reported a market value around $1.5 trillion.

The prospectus also lists risks, including an FTC civil investigative demand, a $1.5 billion copyright settlement, and warnings that losses from an AI catastrophe could exceed insurance capacity. Seven co-founders would hold a share carrying 50.1 percent of votes on ordinary matters. Disclosure: Weekfold's editions are drafted with the help of Claude, which Anthropic makes.

Why it matters

A listing of this size would be among the largest ever and would turn a private AI lab into a company whose finances and risks are public.
