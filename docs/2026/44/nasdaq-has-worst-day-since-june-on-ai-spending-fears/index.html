Nasdaq has worst day since June on AI spending fears

Intel plunged nearly 29 percent and chip stocks tumbled, leaving the Nasdaq down 5.3 percent for October.

Wall Street closed sharply lower on Friday as worries about the cost of the artificial-intelligence build-out sparked a broad sell-off in technology shares. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86 percent, or 747.73 points, to 25,437.00, its biggest one-day percentage drop since 5 June, according to Reuters. The S&P 500 lost 1.82 percent to 7,580.07 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.09 percent to 51,770.31. The Nasdaq now sits 7.8 percent below its record close of 6 October.

Intel led the rout, dropping 28.6 percent after disappointing quarterly results released late Thursday. Yahoo Finance reported that chief executive Lip-Bu Tan blamed a PC slowdown linked to memory prices and availability. Nvidia fell 4.2 percent, AMD 10.9 percent, and the broader chip index 7.2 percent, leaving it down more than 15 percent for the month. Dell and HP fell about 11 and 10 percent. Crypto-linked stocks Coinbase, Strategy and Robinhood dropped between 7.2 and 10.6 percent after reports of a $6.3 billion hack at Binance.

For the month, the Nasdaq lost 5.3 percent, its worst month since March 2025, Yahoo Finance reported, while the S&P 500 fell about 1.2 percent and the Dow gained about 1.5 percent. Oil and energy shares ran the other way: Exxon Mobil rose 2.2 percent and Chevron 2.8 percent after beating profit forecasts, and US crude settled around $104 a barrel.

Investors were also reading the Federal Reserve. Yahoo Finance reported that two Fed officials, St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem and Richmond Fed president Tom Barkin, said rates may need to go higher after the Fed held steady in a split vote this week. Oliver Pursche of Wealthspire Advisors told Reuters: "Those that are falling short are getting punished."

Earnings overall have been strong. Reuters said 82 percent of the 299 S&P 500 companies that have reported beat estimates. Truist's chief investment officer, Keith Lerner, said tech could have downside of roughly 2 to 8 percent from here, while noting that the S&P 500 is still up about 111 percent since October 2022.

Why it matters

The sell-off shows how much of the market's value now rests on AI spending paying off, and how quickly confidence can turn when a bellwether disappoints.
