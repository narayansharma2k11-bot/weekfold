Rays beat Brewers 4-3 in 12 innings, force Game 5

Tampa Bay stayed alive after trailing three games to none, winning on a chopper over first base after a Milwaukee error.

The Tampa Bay Rays avoided a World Series sweep on Tuesday night, beating the Milwaukee Brewers 4-3 in 12 innings at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida, to force a fifth game, according to the Associated Press. Milwaukee had come within one win of its first championship after routing the Rays 10-1 in the previous game.

Milwaukee's Cooper Pratt hit a tiebreaking home run off Ian Seymour in the top of the 12th. The bottom half unravelled after third baseman Joey Ortiz made a throwing error on Junior Caminero's leadoff grounder. Liam Hicks singled, and Jonny DeLuca's single to centre scored pinch-runner Cedric Mullins to tie the game at 3. Chandler Simpson then loaded the bases, and with Milwaukee in a five-man infield, Ben Williamson, who was 1 for 15 in the postseason, chopped a ball over first baseman Jake Bauers into right field to win it.

Tampa Bay became the fifth of 26 teams to trail 3-0 in the World Series and win Game 4, and the previous four all lost Game 5. Of 43 teams to fall behind 3-0 in any best-of-seven postseason series, only the 2004 Boston Red Sox have come back to win. The Brewers (103-59) lead the series 3-1, and Game 5 was scheduled for Wednesday night at Tropicana Field with Milwaukee needing one win to clinch. This edition's sources do not cover the series result.

Why it matters

Even a 3-0 lead is not safe in baseball, and the Brewers' first title chase was extended by at least one more game.
