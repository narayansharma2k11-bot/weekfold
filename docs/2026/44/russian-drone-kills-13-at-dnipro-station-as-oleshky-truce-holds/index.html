Russian drone kills 13 at Dnipro station as Oleshky truce holds

A rare local ceasefire arranged for the occupied town of Oleshky was holding, even as Russia struck a crowded railway platform.

A Russian drone hit a crowded platform at the main railway station in Dnipro on Thursday, killing at least 13 people, according to regional governor Oleksandr Hanzha as cited by Reuters; another roundup put the toll at 14. Ukrainian Railways said people were on the platform despite a warning of heightened danger. Moscow's mayor said Russia downed most of 613 drones heading toward the capital overnight.

Elsewhere on the front, a limited ceasefire around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson region was holding. The White House said First Lady Melania Trump helped broker it, and Russian General Valery Gerasimov said Russian forces would stop firing on the Kherson axis from 4 a.m. Moscow time on Tuesday so that aid could be delivered and civilians could leave. Oleshky had about 24,000 residents before Russia captured it in 2022 and has had no electricity, heat or running water, with mined roads and scarce food and medicine, AP reported. Estimates of how many people remain range from about 2,000 to 6,000. An aid shipment was due on Thursday.

The Wall Street Journal, cited by Foreign Exchanges, also reported that Ukraine stopped striking Russian oil refineries earlier this month after weeks of US pressure, a halt that may not last beyond the US midterms.

Why it matters

The strike shows civilian transport is still a target, while Oleshky is a test of whether small humanitarian truces can work in a war with no wider ceasefire.
