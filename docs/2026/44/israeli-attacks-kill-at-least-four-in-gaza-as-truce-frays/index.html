Israeli attacks kill at least four in Gaza as truce frays

More than a year into the ceasefire, Gaza's health ministry says at least 1,580 Palestinians have been killed since it began.

Israeli attacks killed at least four Palestinians in Gaza on Saturday despite a ceasefire that has been nominally in place for more than a year, Al Jazeera reported, citing Anadolu. Teacher Diaa Abu al-Rous was killed when Israeli shelling struck the Nuseirat refugee camp, al-Awda Hospital confirmed. Arafat al-Arair died of wounds from an earlier strike on a vehicle in Gaza City's Tel al-Hawa district, and two girls died when their tent caught fire at a displacement camp in al-Mawasi, Khan Younis; the cause of the fire was unclear.

Gaza's health ministry says at least six people were killed in the past 48 hours and 26 wounded, and that Israeli attacks have killed at least 1,580 Palestinians and injured 5,711 since the October 2025 ceasefire. Democracy Now! reported earlier in the week that a strike on the Ministry of Economy building in al-Mawasi killed four and wounded at least 15.

The UN human rights office warned on Friday that Israeli forces continue to displace Palestinians and raised concerns of ethnic cleansing as people are pushed into a shrinking area. Al Jazeera said the Israeli military is working to expand control of Gaza to 70 percent, following an order from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in May. Talks on a permanent end to the war under President Trump's plan have stalled: Hamas says it will not fully disarm until Israel withdraws, while Israel says it will not leave until Hamas gives up its weapons.

Why it matters

The steady toll shows how thin the ceasefire is and why the stalled peace plan matters for civilians.
