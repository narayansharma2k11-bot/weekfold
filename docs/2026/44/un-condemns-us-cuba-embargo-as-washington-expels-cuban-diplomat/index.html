UN condemns US Cuba embargo as Washington expels Cuban diplomat

The embargo vote passed with fewer supporters than last year, a day after a clash at the UN led to the diplomat's expulsion.

The UN General Assembly on Wednesday again voted to condemn the US economic embargo of Cuba, for the 34th year. Reuters, via Just Security, reported the tally as 130 in favour, 25 abstentions and 17 against, which is 35 fewer votes in favour than last year as more countries sided with Washington.

Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez Parrilla told the Assembly that the United States has openly threatened to use military force and imposed dozens of new coercive economic measures, according to Democracy Now!, which noted that the Trump administration has repeatedly threatened military intervention to change Cuba's political system and that Cuba has suffered outages, shortages and financial trouble. The embargo dates to 1960.

The vote followed a diplomatic rupture. The United States expelled Cuban UN Mission First Secretary Richard Tur de la Concepción after an altercation on Tuesday at the General Assembly with a US official. US Ambassador Mike Waltz posted video of the exchange, and the State Department said the diplomat was no longer welcome, the Wall Street Journal reported.

Why it matters

A shrinking majority against the embargo, alongside open US threats of force, shows Washington's pressure on Cuba is hardening.
