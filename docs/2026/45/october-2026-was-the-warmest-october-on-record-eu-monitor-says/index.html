October 2026 was the warmest October on record, EU monitor says

Copernicus says 2026 is all but certain to pass 1.5C above pre-industrial levels as a strong El Niño builds.

The EU's Copernicus Climate Change Service said on Friday that October 2026 was the warmest October ever recorded, with an average global surface air temperature of 15.35C, according to Down To Earth. That was 1.75C above the 1850-1900 pre-industrial average and 0.9C above the 1991-2020 average. Reuters reported that it was the third month in a row above 1.5C, after August and September.

Copernicus puts the 2026 annual average between 1.56C and 1.61C above pre-industrial levels and gives about an 80% chance that the year ranks second-warmest, behind 2024, and a 20% chance it ties or beats it. It would be only the second calendar year above 1.5C. The 2023-2026 four-year average is also expected to exceed that mark. The Paris Agreement target refers to a long-term average, so a single year above 1.5C does not by itself breach it.

Oceans were also at records. Sea surface temperature outside the polar regions averaged 20.95C, the highest for any October, and the Niño 3.4 region of the tropical Pacific reached an anomaly of 3.21C, higher than the peaks of earlier strong El Niño events, with the event still intensifying. Samantha Burgess of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts said El Niño is adding heat to a climate already warmed by human activity.

Why it matters

The figures arrive days before the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, and show how far the world is running above the Paris Agreement's 1.5C goal.
