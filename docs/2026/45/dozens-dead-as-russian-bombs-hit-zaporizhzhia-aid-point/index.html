Dozens dead as Russian bombs hit Zaporizhzhia aid point

Ukrainian officials say at least 40 people died and dozens are missing after glide bombs struck a school-turned-shelter in Komyshuvakha.

The death toll from a Russian bombing of an aid distribution point in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region rose to 40 on Wednesday, with dozens of people still missing, according to reports from Reuters and the Australian Associated Press. Reuters described it as one of the deadliest strikes on civilians this year.

The attack hit the village of Komyshuvakha on Monday 2 November. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Russian forces first dropped aerial bombs on an underground school building that was serving as a distribution point as people arrived, then struck the shelter a second time. Classes at the school had been suspended, but it sheltered residents and handed out aid every Monday. Ukraine's national police said the attack included a three-ton FAB bomb fitted with a glide kit; defence intelligence websites say such a weapon carries about 1.4 tons of explosives.

Emergency services said two rescue workers and four police officers were among the dead. Bodycam footage from one of the officers, posted by Ukrainian police and verified by Reuters, shows a warning about incoming glide bombs before a large explosion. Fedorov, co-head of the Zaporizhzhia regional defence council, put the number of missing at 59 on Telegram. Reuters footage from Wednesday showed bodies being loaded into vans while excavators dug beside a large crater and rescuers searched underground. The village lies about 17 km (11 miles) from the nearest Russian positions.

World Central Kitchen, which delivers food and water in the community, described the site as a humanitarian and administrative centre. Its Ukraine response director, Yuliya Stefanyuk, said the strike hit a place where residents were organising help for themselves. Russia's Defence Ministry said it had targeted Ukrainian military units around Komyshuvakha and two other settlements and claimed up to 25 Ukrainian servicemen were killed. Moscow and Kyiv both deny deliberately targeting civilians.

The bombing came a day after a six-day ceasefire expired. That truce, brokered by US first lady Melania Trump, allowed nearly 800 people to be evacuated from the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky, and Zelenskiy awarded her a state honour for the effort. US envoy Steve Witkoff has announced that Washington will host Ukrainian and Russian delegations in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November to discuss ending the war, now in its fifth year, along with postwar security and de-escalation. Reuters later reported that the toll climbed further in the days after, as rescuers continued to search the rubble.

Why it matters

The strike shows how civilians sheltering and collecting aid remain exposed even as new diplomacy gets under way, and it lands days before the Abu Dhabi talks.
