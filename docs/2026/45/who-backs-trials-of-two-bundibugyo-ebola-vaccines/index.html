WHO backs trials of two Bundibugyo Ebola vaccines

Tedros announced that the candidates, from Oxford and Moderna, will be added to a trial led by Congo's biomedical institute.

The World Health Organization said on Wednesday that it is backing clinical trials of two new vaccines against the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, following recommendations from its expert groups. Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus announced the decision at a press conference in Geneva. The candidates are ChAdOx1 BDBV, developed by the University of Oxford with the Serum Institute of India, and Moderna's mRNA-1469; both were designed specifically for this virus. The trial will be led by the Democratic Republic of Congo's National Institute for Biomedical Research with international partners including the WHO.

The outbreak began in Congo in May and, according to Africa CDC figures cited by Al Jazeera and Africanews, has caused 10,444 confirmed cases and more than 5,300 deaths, with a fatality rate above 50 percent. No vaccine or treatment is designed for the strain. A separate trial that began last month uses Ervebo, which is approved for the Zaire strain of Ebola, not Bundibugyo. Al Jazeera reported that results are expected in early 2027; Africanews gave no start dates or efficacy data.

Tedros stressed that vaccines are not a substitute for proven measures such as early care, contact tracing, isolation and safe burials. He also condemned travel restrictions, which Euronews reported have been imposed on travellers from Congo by Ghana and the United States, saying they penalise countries that share information. Al Jazeera added that a British health worker infected with Ebola is being evacuated to the UK, where the government rates the risk to the public as low.

Why it matters

With no vaccine tailored to Bundibugyo, the trials are the first formal route to learning whether any shot can slow what is described as the fastest-growing outbreak in Congo's history.
