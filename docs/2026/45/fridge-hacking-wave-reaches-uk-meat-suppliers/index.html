Fridge-hacking wave reaches UK meat suppliers

Cyberattacks that overheated supermarket refrigeration across Europe also hit a few British firms, officials said.

Hackers who raised temperatures in internet-connected fridges and freezers at supermarkets across Europe also reached UK firms, the Press Association reported on 3 November. Al Jazeera said units at more than 100 shops in several countries overheated on Monday 2 November, including Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg, seven Coop stores in Sweden and 13 SuperValu stores in Ireland. Hundreds of French supermarkets were hit in October. Nobody has claimed responsibility.

In Britain the Association of Independent Meat Suppliers said a few of its roughly 300 members were affected, but the units were empty and no meat was lost. The Record reported the attackers used Copeland XWEB controllers; Copeland confirmed some products are affected, and a firmware fix has been available since August. The National Cyber Security Centre called the national impact minimal and said it is too early to say who was responsible.

The centre's chief executive, Richard Horne, said the UK had done "tech on the cheap" for decades, and warned other systems such as refrigeration, sluice gates and power switching face similar exposure. EU farm commissioner Christophe Hansen called the attacks a deliberate attempt to disrupt food supply, though he said food security was not at risk.

Why it matters

The incidents show how unpatched, internet-facing control systems can be turned against everyday infrastructure, with no sophisticated tools needed.
