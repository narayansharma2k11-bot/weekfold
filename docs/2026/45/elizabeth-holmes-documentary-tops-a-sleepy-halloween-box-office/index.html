Elizabeth Holmes documentary tops a sleepy Halloween box office

A24's three-hour film edged a DC superhero release in a weekend that was among the year's weakest.

"You Can See Everything", a documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, took first place at the North American box office for the first time in its third weekend, according to studio estimates reported by the Associated Press.

It earned an estimated $10.6 million from 2,655 locations, just ahead of Warner Bros.' DC film "Clayface" at $10.4 million, which fell 57 percent in its second weekend. The documentary has made about $63.9 million domestically on a reported $7 million budget. LAIKA's "Wildwood" was third with $7.5 million.

Total ticket sales were estimated at under $56 million, which AP said would be the second-lowest weekend of the year, as Halloween on Saturday kept people away from theatres. Domestic takings remain more than 17 percent above last year. Final figures were due on Monday; industry sites reported slightly different numbers, so the estimates here may shift.

Why it matters

A long, low-budget documentary beating a studio franchise film is a rare result, and a reminder that audiences are still choosing selectively.
