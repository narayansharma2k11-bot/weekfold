Politics · Tuesday 3 November 2026

Democrats seize House and Senate in midterm rebuke of Trump

Voters ended Republican control of Congress on election night, flipping at least seven Senate seats and the House, according to Al Jazeera and other outlets.

Democrats won control of both chambers of the US Congress in the midterm elections held on Tuesday 3 November 2026, ending the Republican trifecta that followed the 2024 election. Al Jazeera reported that the Associated Press called the Senate for Democrats late on Tuesday, and that the House was also called for the party late that night.

The scale became clearer by early Wednesday. Cox Media Group's stations reported Democrats leading 228 to 139 in the House with several races uncalled, after a gain of 19 seats, and on pace for their biggest majority since 2008. In the Senate they stood at 54 to 44 with two contests undecided. Al Jazeera said the outgoing Senate was 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats and two independents who caucus with Democrats, so the party needed a net gain of four seats and had flipped at least seven. Alaska, where Dan Sullivan held a narrow edge over Mary Peltola, and Mississippi, where Democrat Scott Colom was marginally ahead of Cindy Hyde-Smith, were still being counted at that point.

The flipped Senate seats included several that Republicans had held for decades. Al Jazeera listed James Talarico over Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, Sherrod Brown over Sen. Jon Husted in Ohio, Roy Cooper in North Carolina, Adam Hamilton over Sen. Roger Marshall in Kansas, Josh Turek over Ashley Hinson in Iowa, Annie Andrews in South Carolina, and Troy Jackson over Sen. Susan Collins in Maine. Democrats also held Georgia through Jon Ossoff and Virginia through Mark Warner, and Abdul El-Sayed beat Mike Rogers in Michigan. In the House, races reported as Democratic gains included Iowa's 3rd District and Pennsylvania's 10th, where Janelle Stelson defeated Rep. Scott Perry.

Reactions split along predictable lines. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to become Speaker, said voters had "had enough of the extremism, enough of the corruption." Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it a generational remake of the Senate map, while Speaker Mike Johnson said keeping the House would have defied more than a century of midterm history. Al Jazeera reported that President Donald Trump said Democrats had fought hard and suggested the US-Israel war on Iran and higher petrol prices probably played a part, while also posting that he was not on the ballot.

Trump said he holds total veto power and expects Democrats to send him bills he called insane, which he said he would veto, adding that they would come back to negotiate after a few months. The new Congress is scheduled to be sworn in on 3 January 2027, when Democrats can begin using their majorities to stall legislation and hold up nominations. Final tallies, including the close Alaska and Mississippi Senate races and remaining House seats, were still to be settled in the days after election night.

Why it matters

Divided government will reshape what Washington can pass or block for Trump's final two years, and shows how voters judged his economy and Iran policies.
