Gunman kills six in Kraków's Main Square; Poland calls it terrorism

A 24-year-old Polish man opened fire from a car in the city's historic centre, and police later found three relatives dead at his home.

A gunman killed six people and wounded around 15 more on Saturday afternoon in Kraków's Main Market Square, one of Poland's busiest tourist destinations. A tram driver raised the alarm at about 3 p.m., reporting that a man in a moving car had fired at his tram near the square and shattered the cab window, according to Associated Press reporting carried by PBS.

Police said the attacker, a Polish citizen, drove into the square in military-style fatigues and fired a military-type long gun at random people. Euronews reported he was masked and armed with a semi-automatic rifle resembling an AR-15, plus a pistol, and that more weapons and ammunition were found in his car. He was shot and wounded in a gunfight with officers a short distance away, then detained and taken to hospital. Police said he acted alone. Euronews counted six dead and 16 injured, including two police officers and the suspect himself; the AP's regional toll was six dead and 15 wounded, and early reports had put the dead at five.

Euronews said the suspect is 24 and from Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, with no prior convictions and a firearms licence. It reported that Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said he held "very radical views" and was drawn to far-right ideologies, and that Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz denied reports he had served in the armed forces. Police also found the bodies of his father, mother and sister at the family home; they reportedly had gunshot wounds. The victims in the square included four women and two men, one of them a British citizen, with Portuguese and Russian nationals among the injured.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk described the attack as terrorism and named two senior constables, Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański, as the officers who detained the suspect; one officer was reported in serious condition. President Karol Nawrocki said his thoughts were with victims and families, and US Ambassador Tom Rose called it a "savage and murderous attack" on Poland's peace.

Mass shootings are rare in Poland, and Kraków draws around 15 million visitors a year, most passing through the Main Square. Poland declared two days of national mourning from midnight on Sunday, with flags at half-mast and entertainment events cancelled. Investigators have yet to set out a full motive, and the suspect's condition and the exact circumstances of his family's deaths remain subjects of the inquiry.

Why it matters

A rare act of mass violence in a major European tourist city, officially treated as terrorism and linked by ministers to far-right ideology, is likely to shape Poland's debate on extremism and firearms licensing.
