Russell Brand convicted of rape and three sexual assaults in London

The comedian was remanded in custody after a jury at Southwark Crown Court returned mixed verdicts; sentencing is set for Monday.

Russell Brand, 51, was found guilty at Southwark Crown Court of one count of rape and three of sexual assault, and was acquitted of two other rape counts and one attempted indecent assault, according to AP and Australian Associated Press coverage. The jury of eight men and four women deliberated for more than eight hours. The verdicts came on Thursday and dominated coverage on Friday. The convictions relate to incidents between 2004 and 2009, including an alleged rape of a fan at his home in 2009.

Judge Joel Bennathan refused bail, citing the risk that Brand would flee, and said a prison term of some length would follow. Brand denied the charges and told the court he never forced anyone. Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones had called him a ruthless sexual predator, while the defence argued the complainants' accounts were shaped by a 2023 documentary featuring allegations against him. Police have passed prosecutors files on two further rape allegations, AP reported.

Brand rose to fame in the early 2000s through stand-up, television and radio, appeared in Hollywood films and was briefly married to singer Katy Perry. He later built an online following with wellness and religious content.

Why it matters

The verdict closes a case that began with a 2023 media investigation and marks a major reckoning for one of Britain's best-known entertainers.
