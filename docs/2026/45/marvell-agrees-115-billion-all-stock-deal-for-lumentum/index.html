Marvell agrees $115 billion all-stock deal for Lumentum

The chipmaker is betting that optical connectivity will define the next phase of AI data centres, and its investors were not impressed.

Marvell Technology said on Monday it will buy Lumentum in an all-stock transaction with an implied enterprise value of about $115 billion, according to a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Each Lumentum share will convert into 4.5 Marvell shares, or roughly $1,132 per share at announcement, a premium of about 23.9 percent to the unaffected prices of 28 October.

Marvell holders would own about 67 percent of the combined company and Lumentum holders about 33 percent. Marvell chief executive Matt Murphy would stay on as chairman and chief executive, and three Lumentum directors, including chief executive Michael Hurlston, would join the board. The companies said the deal targets a $550 billion market and more than $1 billion in annual cost savings, and expect it to add to Marvell's adjusted earnings per share in the first year. Closing needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected in the first half of 2028.

Markets reacted coolly. The Next Web, citing Reuters, reported Lumentum shares down about 10 percent and Marvell about 5 percent on Monday morning, after Marvell had already fallen sharply since talks were first reported. The site, citing Bloomberg, called it the year's largest deal by equity value.

Why it matters

The deal signals how much money is flowing into the plumbing of AI infrastructure, and the long closing timeline leaves regulators years to weigh in.
