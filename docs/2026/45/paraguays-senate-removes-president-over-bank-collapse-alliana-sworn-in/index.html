Paraguay's Senate removes president over bank collapse; Alliana sworn in

Santiago Peña was ousted and placed under house arrest days after the Ueno Bank failure locked millions out of their savings.

Paraguay's Senate voted on Friday to remove President Santiago Peña from office, and Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in the same day to serve out the term, which ends in August 2028. Al Jazeera reported that 44 of the Senate's 45 members voted for removal, with one abstention; the Associated Press reported that all 44 senators present voted in favour. Peña is the second Paraguayan president removed by impeachment since the end of Alfredo Stroessner's dictatorship in 1989, according to AP.

Shortly after the vote, prosecutors ordered Peña confined to one of his homes in Asunción on suspicion of breach of trust, influence peddling and using public office for personal gain, AP said. He was told to appear before prosecutors on Saturday morning. The offences carry up to ten years in prison, and he denies wrongdoing. An arrest warrant was also issued for Carlos Carvallo, who headed the finance company that became Ueno before Peña made him central bank chief in 2023. At least 27 people face criminal charges in the scandal, Al Jazeera reported.

The crisis began on Tuesday, when the central bank seized Ueno after it fell below solvency requirements. The bank had around three million customers, close to two-thirds of Paraguay's adults, and held roughly $800 million in public money, including about $355 million of state pension funds, according to Al Jazeera. Customers lost access to accounts and pension payments. Peña had been identified as a shareholder in Ueno's parent company in 2023 and says he sold his stake in early 2025.

The sequence moved quickly. On Thursday Peña offered his resignation, but Congress declined it and the lower house voted 78-0 to impeach him. Opposition lawmakers argued that accepting a resignation would have let him keep a seat as a senator for life, with protections against prosecution. In a social media post after his removal, Peña said he was leaving with gratitude and acknowledged the anguish of people still waiting for answers. He did not attend the hearings and sent lawyers instead.

Former president Horacio Cartes, the Colorado Party leader, said the party backed impeachment to restore the country's institutions. Alliana, 52, was vice president under Peña and a former governor of Ñeembucú department who also led the Chamber of Deputies. The lower house declined to open impeachment proceedings against him, though AP reported he is also under investigation in connection with the bank. On taking office he said the country has urgent needs.

Attention now turns to returning depositors' money and to the criminal cases. Congress passed a law this week requiring a state guarantee fund to repay stranded customers within five business days, with payouts that began Thursday, according to AP's earlier reporting, and prosecutors say their investigations will continue.

Why it matters

A president once welcomed in Washington as a regional ally has been removed within days by a bank failure that hit most of the adult population, testing Paraguay's institutions and its recently improved credit standing.
