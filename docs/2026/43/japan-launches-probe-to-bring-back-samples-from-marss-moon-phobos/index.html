Japan launches probe to bring back samples from Mars's moon Phobos

The MMX spacecraft lifted off on an H3 rocket and reached its planned orbit, starting a mission meant to return samples in 2031.

Japan launched its Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe on an H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center at 4:41 a.m. on Tuesday, 20 October, Japan time (9:41 p.m. in Central Europe on Monday). About 1 hour 11 minutes after liftoff the spacecraft reached its planned orbit, and mission leader Yasuhiro Kawakatsu said it was in a stable condition, according to Nippon.com.

The plan is to travel to Mars, land on Phobos, the larger of the planet's two moons, collect surface samples and bring them back to Earth in 2031. If it succeeds, MMX will be the first mission to return samples from a Martian moon and the first round trip to the Martian system. The European Space Agency says MMX will be the first spacecraft to orbit Phobos and expects the probe to reach Mars next summer.

JAXA leads the mission, with partners including NASA, France's CNES, Germany's DLR and ESA. ESA supplied the spacecraft's deep-space communications equipment and ground-station support through its Estrack network, and European scientists sit on the science team. H3 project manager Makoto Arita said the launch opened the first chapter of the mission, and Nippon.com noted the H3 had now flown three consecutive successful launches.

Why it matters

Samples from Phobos could help settle whether the moons are captured asteroids or debris from a giant impact on Mars, and test the technology needed for future sample returns.
