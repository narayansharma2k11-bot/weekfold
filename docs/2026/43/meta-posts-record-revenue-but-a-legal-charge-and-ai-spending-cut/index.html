Meta posts record revenue, but a legal charge and AI spending cut profit

Third-quarter revenue rose 26 percent to $64.6 billion while free cash flow turned negative and the company announced a new class of non-voting shares.

Meta Platforms reported third-quarter revenue of $64.60 billion, up 26 percent from a year earlier and above analysts' expectation of about $63.3 billion. Net income was $9.86 billion, or $3.83 a share, after a $9.89 billion legal charge tied to a settlement with 52 state attorneys general; excluding that charge, earnings would have been $7.40 a share. Operating margin fell to 20 percent from 40 percent as costs rose 68 percent.

Spending on artificial intelligence was the other drag. Capital expenditure reached $34.91 billion, up about 80 percent, and exceeded operating cash flow of $32.79 billion, leaving free cash flow at negative $2.12 billion. Meta narrowed its 2026 capex forecast to $135 billion to $145 billion and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73 billion to $76 billion. It said daily active people averaged 3.64 billion in September, and that more than 10 million people in the United States and Canada set up accounts for its Muse personal agent in about six weeks.

Meta will also issue non-voting Class C shares as a dividend, one for each Class A or B share, which works like a two-for-one split. The change takes effect once an amended certificate of incorporation becomes effective on 19 January 2027. SiliconANGLE reported that Mark Zuckerberg keeps about 60.6 percent of the vote on a 13.4 percent economic stake. Shares rose in after-hours trading, according to XTB, and SiliconANGLE reported they fell almost 8 percent the following day as investors weighed the AI spending.

Why it matters

Meta's results show how much of big tech's profit is being reinvested in AI infrastructure, and the new share class lets it raise money without diluting Zuckerberg's control.
