Drone strike on Abyei market kills at least 153 people

The toll from Monday's attack in the disputed Sudan-South Sudan border zone more than doubled as fires slowed recovery of bodies.

A drone strike on a market in Abyei, a disputed and oil-rich area on the border between Sudan and South Sudan, has killed at least 153 people, according to the region's caretaker information minister, Yohana Akol Ngor. The strike hit around midday on Monday. Ngor said the toll had risen from an initial 73 and could climb further because fires were hampering the recovery of bodies. About 8,000 people have been displaced, and Médecins Sans Frontières said it was treating more than 20 wounded, some with burns, at Ameth Bek Hospital.

Responsibility is contested. South Sudanese officials, including Ngor, blamed Sudan's army but offered no evidence, and the army did not immediately respond. Reuters, citing three diplomatic sources, an aid worker and a conflict expert, reported that the strike hit a fuel depot the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) had used to supply their fighters. Sudan's military-led government says the RSF is active in parts of Abyei, which Ngor denies. Reuters could not verify either account, and the RSF did not respond to requests for comment. South Sudan's foreign ministry summoned the Sudanese ambassador in protest, while saying South Sudan has not taken sides in the war.

Both countries claim Abyei, which borders Sudan's Kordofan region, a major centre of fighting. The UN peacekeeping force there, UNISFA, warned in May that the RSF's presence had worsened insecurity. Sudan's war between the army and the RSF began in April 2023 and has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Drone strikes have escalated recently. Attacks hit Nyala and Khartoum airport this week, and a group called Emergency Lawyers attributed dozens of deaths in al-Muglad on 17 and 18 October to the army. A 10 October air strike near Sudan's border with Egypt killed more than 70 artisanal miners, according to a local rights group, and Chad has accused Sudan's military of drone strikes on its territory. An earlier tally of 73 dead in Abyei was reported in a 20 October security brief from the Foundation for Defense of Democracies. Democracy Now separately reported that a drone strike near a market in West Kordofan, in an area the RSF controls, killed 80 people on Sunday, one of the deadliest single strikes since the war began. The RSF blamed the army. More than 13 million people have been displaced by the war and 33.7 million need aid, according to that report.

Why it matters

The strike widens Sudan's war toward a fragile border zone claimed by two states and raises the risk of a wider regional confrontation.
