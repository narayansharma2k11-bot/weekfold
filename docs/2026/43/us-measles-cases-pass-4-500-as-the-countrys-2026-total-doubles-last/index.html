Health · Thursday 22 October 2026

US measles cases pass 4,500 as the country's 2026 total doubles last year's

CDC data show 4,588 confirmed cases, though growth slowed to 4 percent for the week.

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention counted 4,588 confirmed measles cases in 2026 as of 22 October, up 178, or 4.0 percent, from 4,410 a week earlier, according to Contagion Live. The weekly rise was slower than the previous week's 8.1 percent, but the year's total is roughly double the 2,289 cases recorded in all of 2025. Cases span 47 jurisdictions, and 43 outbreaks have started this year. About 96 percent of cases are linked to outbreaks.

Hospitalizations reached 479, about 10 percent of cases, and 58 percent of patients are under 20. Some 96 percent of 2026 cases were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status. The CDC lists three measles deaths this year, a figure that may change as reviews finish, while a Pennsylvania coroner's report on 13 October described a sixth measles-related death in that state. Reuters reported on 16 October that Pennsylvania had 1,260 cases and 238 hospitalizations, that New York had declared a disaster emergency over the outbreak, and that the CDC had begun using National Center for Health Statistics mortality data to count measles deaths.

Kindergarten coverage with the MMR vaccine has fallen from 95.2 percent in 2019-20 to 92.4 percent in 2025-26, leaving about 280,000 kindergartners at risk, according to Contagion Live. The CDC counts only confirmed cases reported by states, so the true total may be higher.

Why it matters

Falling vaccination rates are allowing measles to spread again and CDC data show the outbreak is still growing, even if more slowly.
