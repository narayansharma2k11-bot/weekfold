Saudi Arabia postpones 'Davos in the Desert' to 2027 amid Houthi attacks

The Future Investment Initiative, due in Riyadh from 26 to 29 October, has been pushed back with no new date set.

Saudi Arabia has postponed its flagship Future Investment Initiative, nicknamed "Davos in the Desert", to 2027, with a new date to be announced. The tenth edition had been scheduled to run in Riyadh from 26 to 29 October. The conference's chief executive said the decision came from a commitment to partners, members, speakers and delegates. Al Jazeera reported that Ruth Porat of Alphabet, David Solomon of Goldman Sachs and Laurence Fink of BlackRock had been among the expected speakers.

The move follows weeks of Houthi attacks on the kingdom. Al Jazeera put the toll at no fewer than 18 killed and hundreds injured, with military, energy and civilian sites hit, including Riyadh's international airport. Riyadh government agencies told staff to work from home, and the 99-storey Kingdom Centre, which houses the IMF's regional office, asked workers to stay away. A 20 October security brief from the Foundation for Defense of Democracies said the airport was restricting air traffic. Fighting in Yemen has displaced more than 233,000 people, according to Al Jazeera.

Why it matters

The cancellation of the Gulf's biggest investor gathering shows the Yemen conflict is now hurting Saudi Arabia's economic showcase, not only its security.
