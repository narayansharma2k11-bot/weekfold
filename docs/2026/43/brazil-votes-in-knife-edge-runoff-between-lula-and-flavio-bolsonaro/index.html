Brazil votes in knife-edge runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

Final polls show a near dead heat as nearly 160 million eligible voters choose between the veteran president and the son of the jailed ex-president.

Brazilians went to the polls on Sunday to choose between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, who is seeking a fourth non-consecutive term for the Workers' Party, and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, of the Liberal Party. Polls opened at 8 a.m. local time (11:00 GMT) and close at 5 p.m. (20:00 GMT), with nearly 160 million people eligible to vote. No result was available when this edition was compiled.

The first round on 4 October was close: Bolsonaro took 47.03 percent of valid votes to Lula's 45.16 percent, according to the Rio Times, and neither passed 50 percent. Final polls showed a virtual tie. Futura had Bolsonaro at 50.1 percent and Lula at 49.9, while Datafolha and Quaest each had Bolsonaro at 51 and Lula at 49, and Palver had it 50 to 49. Al Jazeera noted that an AtlasIntel survey earlier in the campaign gave Bolsonaro 49.8 percent to Lula's 47.6, and that Lula had narrowed the gap since.

Bolsonaro promises a militarised approach to crime, closer partnership with the Trump administration, a debt ceiling and tighter control of inflation and spending. He has said he would redemocratise Brazil by reforming the constitution and has pledged to fight for the release of his father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year sentence for a coup plot after losing the 2022 election. Lula points to falling unemployment and poverty and reduced Amazon deforestation, but rising household debt and a polarised political landscape have limited his reform agenda.

The campaign has been bitter. The candidates held their only debate on 18 October. Protesters rallied outside Meta's offices in São Paulo accusing the company of interfering in the election, and Brazil's Electoral Court gave Meta 24 hours to prove it treats both campaigns equally, Democracy Now reported. A corruption scandal involving banker Daniel Vorcaro has implicated figures across the political spectrum, including Jair Bolsonaro, according to Al Jazeera. Large pro-Lula rallies have been held, and there are concerns about US interference, Al Jazeera reported. Results are expected soon after polls close.

Why it matters

The result will decide whether Latin America's largest country stays with a left-wing government or turns toward a Trump-aligned right, and could embolden or deflate similar movements across the region.
