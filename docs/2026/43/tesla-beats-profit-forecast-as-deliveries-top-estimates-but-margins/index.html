Tesla beats profit forecast as deliveries top estimates but margins stay thin

Revenue was flat at $28.05 billion, and AI and robotics spending pushed operating income down 56 percent.

Tesla reported third-quarter revenue of $28.05 billion, flat on the year and in line with expectations, according to Electrek's account of the results. Adjusted earnings were 48 cents a share, ahead of the 45 cents analysts expected but 4 percent lower than a year earlier. GAAP net income fell 43 percent to $787 million, and operating income dropped 56 percent to $718 million because operating expenses rose 39 percent, with research and development up 69 percent on AI and Optimus robot spending.

Deliveries were 486,532 vehicles, down 2.1 percent from a year earlier but above the roughly 462,000 analysts expected, Tesla said in a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Production was 464,391 vehicles and the company deployed 13.7 gigawatt-hours of energy storage. Total gross margin improved to 19.6 percent from 18.0 percent a year earlier. Free cash flow was $1.53 billion, down from $3.99 billion.

Tesla said flat auto volumes reflected battery supply constraints and reported its largest passenger-vehicle order backlog in four years. Electrek reported that unsupervised full self-driving slipped to the second quarter of 2027 and will be limited to robotaxi zones, and that the company's robotaxi service had logged nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles.

Why it matters

Margins improved but profit fell as Tesla pours money into AI and robots, and the slip in unsupervised driving pushes back one of its most heavily promised products.
