World · Saturday 24 October 2026

Hundreds of thousands march across France as nearly 1,000 are arrested

A week after rioting and looting, Saturday's protests over living costs passed with clashes in several cities under a 70,000-strong police deployment.

Hundreds of thousands of people took part in protests across France on Saturday over living costs, public services and school conditions. The interior ministry estimated 410,000 marchers nationwide, including 110,000 in Paris, while the CGT union said the figures were nearly double. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said police arrested nearly 1,000 people nationwide, including 64 in connection with the Paris demonstration, six of them minors.

Most marches were calm in the early hours, but clashes broke out later. In Paris police used tear gas and water cannon at the end of the march, and authorities said about 500 "rioters" clashed with officers, a lower level of violence than the previous weekend. Tear gas was also fired in Lyon, Clermont-Ferrand, Grenoble, Lille and Nice, there were clashes in Marseille, and police in Dijon were targeted with fireworks. More than 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed, about 20 percent more than a week earlier, and at least 24 metro stations were closed. The Louvre and Musée d'Orsay shut for the day.

Saturday followed a far more violent weekend. More than half a million people marched on 17 October, over 2,000 people were taken into custody, and Fox News reported about 3,000 shops were damaged or looted. Nuñez had offered to resign, according to Democracy Now, but President Emmanuel Macron declined and blamed seditious ultra-left groups and criminals. Macron then said only union demonstrations would be authorised for the weekend, and local authorities banned marches in Lyon, Nice, Montpellier and Rouen.

Rights groups objected. Amnesty International described the restrictions as a slide toward authoritarian practice, and Human Rights Watch voiced alarm at reports of children severely injured by crowd-control weapons. Macron met high school student representatives on Friday and promised more teachers and an overhaul of the Parcoursup university admissions system, but students said it did not defuse tensions.

The movement has called another protest for next Saturday. Al Jazeera reported that Marine Le Pen and Jordan Bardella were expected to launch their presidential campaign on Sunday with a speech to about 10,000 supporters, and that a counter-protest of 20,000 to 30,000 people was expected in Orléans against the National Rally's congress.

Why it matters

The unrest is testing Macron's government at the moment the far right begins its presidential campaign.
