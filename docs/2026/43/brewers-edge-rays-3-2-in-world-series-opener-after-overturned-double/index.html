Brewers edge Rays 3-2 in World Series opener after overturned double play

Milwaukee led from the first inning, and a replay reversal let Tampa Bay score in the ninth before the Brewers closed it out.

The Milwaukee Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 3-2 in Game 1 of the World Series on Friday 23 October at American Family Field to take a 1-0 lead. Christian Yelich hit an RBI triple in the first inning, in his first World Series game, and William Contreras drove in the second run with a single. Jackson Chourio drove in a run in the seventh to make it 3-1.

Brewers starter Jacob Misiorowski threw five innings, allowing one run on four hits with three strikeouts, and CBS Sports reported his first pitch of 103.5 mph was the fastest in World Series history in the pitch-tracking era. Tampa Bay's starter Nick Martinez also worked five innings with five strikeouts. The Rays' lower order went 1 for 17 with six strikeouts, and Jonathan Aranda was 3 for 3 and scored their first run.

The ninth inning was chaotic. Closer Trevor Megill loaded the bases with one out, and Liam Hicks appeared to ground into a game-ending double play, but replay overturned the call at second base, with CBS Sports noting the fielder did not hold the bag. A run scored to make it 3-2, and Megill then struck out Jonny DeLuca to end it. Game 2 was set for Saturday night in Milwaukee.

Why it matters

Winning the opener gives Milwaukee early control of a series in which Tampa Bay went hitless with runners in scoring position.
