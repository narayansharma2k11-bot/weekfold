Serbia votes in snap election as Vučić faces a student-led challenge

The president turned prime-ministerial candidate leads some polls, but others put the student list ahead after two years of protests.

Serbia held a snap parliamentary election on Sunday, called by President Aleksandar Vučić in September. Vučić, who has led the country as prime minister and then president for more than a decade, resigned the presidency to run for prime minister at the head of his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS). He is challenged by the Student List, a movement of about 250 mostly unfamiliar candidates, among them professors, actors and farmers, led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanović.

The race is tight. An Ipsos poll published on Thursday gave the SNS 47 percent and the students 40 percent, though other polls put the students ahead, according to Al Jazeera. Polling stations were open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., with first results expected about two hours after closing.

The Student List grew out of nearly two years of protests over corruption, triggered by the collapse of a canopy at Novi Sad's railway station in 2024, which killed 16 people. About 150,000 supporters rallied in central Belgrade on 22 October. Jacobin reported the list is targeting undecided voters, past abstainers and the diaspora, and noted irregularities and sporadic violence against opponents. Rights groups say repression has worsened. Vučić and his party deny accusations of repression, corruption and spying on opponents, and he claims the students are funded by foreign agents in Croatia and Montenegro, which they deny.

Vučić is promising higher pensions and public-sector pay, healthcare investment and subsidies for young couples. He has balanced ties with China and Russia while keeping the door open to EU membership, though relations with Brussels have frayed over judicial reform and the rule of law.

Why it matters

A loss for Vučić would end more than a decade of dominant rule in a candidate EU member, while a win would test whether the student movement can sustain pressure.
