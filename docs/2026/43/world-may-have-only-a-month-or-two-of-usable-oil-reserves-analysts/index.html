World may have only a month or two of usable oil reserves, analysts warn

With Brent near $111 and the US reserve at its lowest since 1982, a renewed Hormuz closure would hit stockpiles already drawn down.

Brent crude rose more than $2 to $110.85 a barrel on Wednesday, near a five-month peak, as fears grew of a renewed US-Iran war. Before the conflict began, about 20 million barrels a day, roughly a fifth of global supply, passed through the Strait of Hormuz. Al Jazeera reported that Kpler estimates a full Hormuz closure combined with a blocked Bab al-Mandeb could cut Middle Eastern crude exports from 16.8 million to 7.5 million barrels a day.

Emergency stocks are thin. The International Energy Agency's 32 members agreed on 11 March to release 400 million barrels, with the United States pledging 172.2 million and Japan 79.8 million. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen to 283 million barrels, its lowest since October 1982, and much of the oil was lent and must be returned with extra barrels. Global strategic stocks outside China and Iran have fallen nearly 327 million barrels since the end of December 2025, and analyst Aaron Kildow estimates only about 400 million barrels of usable reserves remain, nearly half of them in Japan.

IG Group's Chris Beauchamp said the world may have only a month or two of reserves if Hormuz shuts again, and called $150 a barrel a very real possibility. The IEA says diesel is already nearly double the price of crude. China holds 1.13 billion barrels in onshore stocks, about 80 days of refinery intake, while India's reserves cover about eight days of imports. Separately, the IEA has warned that Europe faces a difficult winter with low gas reserves, Al Jazeera reported.

Why it matters

Thin reserves leave importers, especially in Asia and Europe, with little cushion if the Gulf's main export route closes again.
