Oslo talks on Ukraine end without a deal as Witkoff heads to Putin

Zelenskyy met US envoy Steve Witkoff and European leaders in Norway, with Washington calling the talks constructive but no agreement reached.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy held talks in Oslo on Tuesday with US special envoy Steve Witkoff and European leaders, aiming to move the war with Russia toward negotiations. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre said Zelenskyy had asked Witkoff and Jared Kushner to take part, and Kushner joined remotely because of illness. Finland was represented at leader level, and European and NATO officials also attended.

A US official described the talks as constructive and said they made "meaningful progress" toward a coordinated approach to dealing with Russia. Topics included security guarantees for Ukraine, a post-war economic plan and Europe's wider security architecture. Witkoff wants to open a path to direct trilateral and multilateral negotiations and is due to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg. Finland's president said the peace process was back on track. No agreement was reached, and neither a Witkoff-Putin date nor a three-way leaders' meeting has been set.

Relations between Washington and Kyiv have worsened. President Donald Trump has publicly criticised Zelenskyy, accused him of not wanting to end the war and suggested someone else should negotiate for Ukraine. Kyiv says the United States has withheld military support and intelligence, which Washington denies. A source said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had not approved Ukraine's latest Patriot interceptor requests, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio denied any deliberate policy change. Zelenskyy said he discussed guarantees modelled on NATO's Article 5 and Patriot supplies with Rubio, and that Ukraine is again receiving early warnings of Russian missile attacks.

A separate dispute concerns Starlink: Ukraine's access was disrupted, Hegseth said it had not been pulled back, and the Wall Street Journal reported Elon Musk ordered the slowdown after consulting Trump and administration officials. Zelenskyy has also called the US waiver of sanctions on Russian diesel exports a reward for Putin.

The fighting continued. A Russian drone hit the highway linking Kyiv and Odesa, killing three people and injuring four, and Russian strikes on three energy facilities of the company DTEK in the Odesa region left more than 32,000 families without power. In Russia's Tambov region, a Ukrainian drone attack started a fire at an oil pumping station, according to the governor.

Why it matters

With US support in question and Russian strikes on Ukraine's grid intensifying, the talks decide whether Europe can hold a coordinated line before winter.
