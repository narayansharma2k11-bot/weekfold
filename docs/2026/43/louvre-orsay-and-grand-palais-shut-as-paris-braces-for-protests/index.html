Louvre, Orsay and Grand Palais shut as Paris braces for protests

France's culture ministry closed leading museums on Saturday, and Art Basel Paris cancelled its weekend programme after opening to the public on Friday.

The Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay, the Picasso Museum and the Grand Palais were all closed on Saturday 24 October, according to a Culture Ministry statement to the Associated Press. The ministry told institutions under its authority to take the steps needed for visitor safety, staff protection and the preservation of artworks, after the previous weekend's protests turned violent with fires, looting and clashes with police. Al Jazeera said the Louis Vuitton Foundation also closed.

Art Basel Paris, which opened to the public only on Friday at the Grand Palais, cancelled its weekend programme, affecting 211 galleries, ABC News reported. About 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed nationwide for the demonstrations over the cost of living, with helicopters, armoured vehicles and water cannon, and some marches were banned in Lyon, Nice and Montpellier.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned that police would be uncompromising with anyone involved in violence. Rights groups criticised the restrictions: Amnesty International described them as a slide toward authoritarian practice, according to Al Jazeera.

Why it matters

Closing the world's most-visited museum shows how far the unrest has spread into daily life and into France's cultural calendar.
