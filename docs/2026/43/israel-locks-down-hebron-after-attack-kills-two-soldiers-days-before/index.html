Conflict · Friday 23 October 2026

Israel locks down Hebron after attack kills two soldiers days before election

Netanyahu ordered the West Bank city sealed and prepared wider closures as the army launched a multi-day operation and arrests mounted.

Two Israeli soldiers were killed in a shooting attack at a checkpoint in northern Hebron, in the occupied West Bank, Al Jazeera reported on Friday, prompting Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to order the city closed and to prepare a lockdown of the wider territory. The attack targeted a reserve battalion near the settlement of Telem. The military said it killed the two Palestinian gunmen, and two Palestinians were detained afterwards. Israel's ambulance service first reported at least five soldiers injured, two of whom later died. Witnesses said the shooting lasted about 20 minutes, which complicated evacuations.

Netanyahu called the response a reaction to a prevented "major attack" and cancelled election events, with Israel voting on Tuesday 27 October. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for full Israeli control of the West Bank and for the Palestinian Authority to be dismantled and demilitarised. Hamas, Palestinian Islamic Jihad and the PFLP praised the attack and called for more confrontations. The attack came as Israel prepared for its first election since the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks; Democracy Now reported earlier in the week that President Trump had endorsed Netanyahu and that most polls showed his coalition short of a majority.

By Saturday, the Times of Israel reported, the army had launched a major operation in Hebron expected to last several days. Defence Minister Israel Katz said about 2,000 Palestinians had been displaced, Palestinians reported widespread closures and blocked roads, and the army had begun demolishing structures near the attack site.

Violence elsewhere in the territory also drew attention. On Thursday Israeli forces arrested 36 Palestinians across the West Bank, according to Wafa citing the Palestinian Prisoner's Society. The Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission documented 17 attacks by soldiers and settlers on farmers during the olive harvest that day, including assaults, burned trees and live fire. US congressional candidate Brad Lander was briefly detained by the Israeli military on Wednesday while volunteering in the harvest.

The week began with settler attacks on harvesters. Democracy Now reported that about 80 masked settlers attacked Palestinians in Abu Falah, seriously injuring Washington Post photojournalist Heidi Levine, whom they stabbed and robbed. US Ambassador Mike Huckabee called the attack terror, Netanyahu called reports of settler violence a smear campaign, and Israeli police detained 10 suspects.

Why it matters

The crackdown lands days before an election in which security is a central issue, and it risks deepening a West Bank already strained by settler violence.
