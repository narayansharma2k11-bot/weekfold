Kenyan cult leader convicted over starvation deaths of 190 children

A Mombasa court found preacher Paul Mackenzie and 28 others guilty of murder over the Shakahola Forest deaths, with sentencing set for 6 November.

A court in Mombasa on Monday convicted Kenyan preacher Paul Mackenzie and 28 others of murder over the deaths of 191 people, 190 of them children, in one of the worst cult-linked tragedies in recent memory. Judge Diana Kavedza Mochache found that Mackenzie's group ran an organised system of religious radicalisation and enforced fasting that led to the deaths, and she rejected the defence argument that the victims had fasted voluntarily.

Prosecutors said Mackenzie, who led the Good News International Church, told followers that starving themselves and their children would carry them to heaven before the world ended. They described the plan as staged in phases, starting with the children. The judge said some children were isolated and denied food while their parents looked on, and Mackenzie reportedly also banned schooling and hospital care for his followers.

The case dates to 2023, when a tip-off led police to arrest Mackenzie and to find bodies in shallow graves in Shakahola Forest, near Kenya's southeastern coast. Authorities have since recovered more than 400 bodies, many still unidentified; Associated Press reporting said only 26 of the children had been identified. Police had been monitoring Mackenzie since 2017, and his church was shut down in 2019, after which he moved to the forest with followers.

Relatives of victims welcomed the outcome. Francis Wanje, who lost eight family members, said he was happy and had not expected a guilty verdict after such a long case. Director of Public Prosecutions Renson Ingonga called the convictions a significant achievement given the number of counts, accused people and witnesses involved. Defence lawyer Lawrence Obonyo said his clients were surprised and disappointed and would appeal.

Monday's ruling covered 191 murder counts. Mackenzie still faces other charges, including terrorism-related counts that he denies, and has pleaded not guilty in a separate case over 52 deaths in Kwa Binzaro village. Other deaths linked to the forest are the subject of separate proceedings. Sentencing for all 29 convicted people is expected on 6 November, and the defence says it has been instructed to appeal, so the legal process is far from over.

Why it matters

The verdict is a landmark for Kenya's courts and a test of how the country regulates fringe religious groups and the long gap between early police monitoring of Mackenzie from 2017 and his arrest in 2023.
