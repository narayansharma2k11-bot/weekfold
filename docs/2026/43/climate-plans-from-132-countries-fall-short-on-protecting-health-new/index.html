Climate plans from 132 countries fall short on protecting health, new scorecard finds

The Global Climate and Health Alliance ranks Colombia first and finds India, Japan and New Zealand at the bottom.

A new scorecard from the Global Climate and Health Alliance, a coalition of NGOs, assessed how well 132 countries' national climate plans protect health. Each plan was scored across eight indicators, including air pollution, healthcare-sector action, a just transition and financing, for a maximum of 32 points. Colombia topped the ranking with 29 points, followed by Nigeria with 25 and Cambodia with 24. The European Union ranked 12th, and 19 of the top 20 scorers were low- or middle-income economies.

Down To Earth reported that India scored 2 out of 32, joint bottom with Japan and New Zealand, because its plan does not mention air pollution. Only five countries, Cambodia, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica and Paraguay, set black carbon reduction targets. The EU was the only major emitter with a specific air pollution target, a phase-out of hydrofluorocarbons by 2050. The United States scored 7 because its plan was not considered active after its withdrawal from the Paris Agreement.

Euronews reported that health ministries were closely involved in drawing up national climate plans in only 15 percent of the countries analysed. The alliance said governments cannot claim to put health first without tackling fossil fuel dependence, and it pointed to COP31 in November as a chance to turn the findings into more ambitious policy.

Why it matters

Air pollution and heat already cost lives, so climate plans that ignore health leave governments with weaker arguments and fewer allies for tougher action.
