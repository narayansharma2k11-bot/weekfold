Alberta voters reject a path toward independence from Canada

Roughly three in four voters backed staying in Canada, a boost for Prime Minister Mark Carney, though separatists say the movement will continue.

Voters in the Canadian province of Alberta overwhelmingly rejected a move toward independence in a referendum. With more than 1.8 million votes counted, unofficial Elections Alberta figures reported by ITV showed about 75 percent in favour of remaining in Canada, while Al Jazeera's count put the figure near 76 percent. The ballot was not a direct vote on independence: it asked whether to hold another referendum on the subject, and a yes would have started a path toward a future separation vote.

Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed what he called a clear choice for unity. He said the roughly quarter of voters who backed the proposal should not automatically be counted as independence supporters, because some used the vote to express frustration with Ottawa, and he called for both sides to work together. Premier Danielle Smith, a conservative who called the referendum, said she voted against independence and that Albertans had chosen a stronger Alberta in a united Canada.

Separatists said the result would not end their campaign. Jeffrey Rath of the Alberta Prosperity Project said the push would absolutely continue and ITV reported he wants Smith removed as leader of her United Conservative Party. Smith has said a federalist win should close the secession question. The vote drew outside interest, with US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent calling Alberta a natural partner for Washington, according to Al Jazeera, while Carney is pursuing a new pipeline to export more Alberta bitumen overseas.

Why it matters

The result removes, for now, a major threat to Canadian unity, but the quarter of voters who wanted change signals lasting grievances in the oil-producing west.
