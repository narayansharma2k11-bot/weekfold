US readies sweeping sanctions against the International Criminal Court itself

Washington is preparing to sanction the court as an institution, a step that could hobble its finances and daily operations.

The Trump administration has prepared sanctions targeting the International Criminal Court as a whole and plans to announce them soon, two sources told Reuters, following a report in the Wall Street Journal. The measures would bar US citizens and companies from providing money, goods or services to the court without a Treasury licence, after a grace period of six to seven months. Until now, the US has sanctioned more than a dozen individual ICC judges and prosecutors, but never the institution.

ICC leaders have warned that court-wide sanctions could disrupt IT and insurance contracts, the hiring of investigators and routine payments, including salaries for American staff. Washington, which has never joined the court, objects to its arrest warrants for Israeli leaders and a past investigation involving US personnel in Afghanistan. Secretary of State Marco Rubio launched a campaign in July urging countries to quit the court.

Why it matters

Sanctioning the world's permanent war crimes court as an institution could cripple international accountability for genocide and war crimes.
