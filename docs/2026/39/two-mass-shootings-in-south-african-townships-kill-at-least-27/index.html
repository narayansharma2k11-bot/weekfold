Two mass shootings in South African townships kill at least 27

Gunmen attacked a tavern near Johannesburg and a store near Cape Town within hours of each other, in one of the country's deadliest weekends of gun violence.

At least 27 people were killed and more than 20 wounded in two mass shootings in separate South African townships, police said on Sunday. A day later, the toll rose to 28 when another victim died in hospital. No arrests had been announced in either case.

The first attack happened at about 9:30pm on Saturday at a tavern in Wedela, southwest of Johannesburg. Police said eight gunmen armed with AK-47 rifles and pistols shot at people outside and then inside, killing 17 people and wounding 15. The attackers searched some customers and took their phones before fleeing in two vehicles, and two cars were set on fire outside. About four hours later, gunmen killed 10 people and wounded 11 at a store linked to an entertainment venue in Lwandle, near Cape Town.

Soldiers were sent to Wedela on Sunday, and detectives and specialist units were assigned to both cases. A resident told the AP that earlier local shootings had involved illegal mining gangs but nothing on this scale. Police have opened 17 murder cases and 15 attempted murder cases for Wedela alone.

The attacks came five days after gunmen killed at least 11 people, including a pregnant woman, at a house party in KwaMakhutha near Durban. Police statistics show 5,427 murders in the three months to 30 June, about 60 a day, with shootings the most common cause. Political parties and gun owners estimate that between 2 million and 6 million illegal firearms are circulating.

In March, President Cyril Ramaphosa deployed soldiers to help police in a year-long anti-crime operation in high-crime areas, calling gang crime the biggest threat to South Africa's democracy and economy. The latest killings will add pressure on the government to show results.

Why it matters

South Africa has one of the world's highest murder rates, and gang violence threatens investment and stability in Africa's most industrialised economy.
