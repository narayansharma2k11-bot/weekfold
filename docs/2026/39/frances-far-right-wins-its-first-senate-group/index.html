France's far right wins its first Senate group

Marine Le Pen's National Rally and allies won a record 14 seats in indirect Senate elections, enough to form a formal caucus.

The National Rally and its allies won at least 14 seats in France's 348-seat Senate in Sunday's indirect elections, roughly four times their previous number and enough to form a parliamentary group for the first time. Group status brings more speaking time, staff funding and the power to set up inquiries. Marine Le Pen called it a historic victory.

Senators are chosen by local elected officials, a system that favours traditional parties. The centre-right Republicans remained the largest force with at least 127 seats, and party president Bruno Retailleau was re-elected. Jordan Bardella said the RN senators would ally with the UDR. Le Pen leads polls for next year's presidential election.

Why it matters

The far right's advance into France's upper house is another sign of its growing strength ahead of the 2027 presidential race in the EU's second-largest economy.
