Putin's United Russia claims record Duma supermajority in wartime vote

Preliminary results give the Kremlin's ruling party 355 of 450 seats after a three-day election held with anti-war parties excluded and under a barrage of Ukrainian drones.

President Vladimir Putin's United Russia party won 355 of the 450 seats in the State Duma, Russia's lower house of parliament, according to preliminary results released on Monday by election chief Ella Pamfilova. The total beats the party's previous high of 343 seats from 2016 and hands it a supermajority. Pamfilova said more than 95 per cent of ballots had been counted and put turnout at just over 59 per cent, with final figures due later in the week.

The Communist Party came a distant second with 40 seats, followed by the nationalist LDPR with 25 and New People with 20. Early party-list tallies reported by TASS had United Russia at close to 58 per cent of the vote. More than 3.6 million people voted online, and ballots were also cast in Crimea and four Ukrainian regions Moscow claims to have annexed, which the UN and most countries do not recognise as Russian.

The vote, held from 18 to 20 September, was the first parliamentary election since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. No party critical of Putin took part. Yabloko, the only registered party openly opposed to the war, was struck off by the Supreme Court in August, and its leader Nikolai Rybakov spoiled his own ballot with a message calling for peace.

Ukraine called the election illegitimate, especially in occupied territory. Moscow accused Kyiv of trying to disrupt the vote after a huge wave of drone attacks on Sunday that TASS described as the biggest of the war. Al Jazeera, citing Russian officials, reported three deaths in the Moscow region, two in Russian-held Kherson including an election official, and damage at a Moscow oil refinery.

Analysts at the Berlin-based think tank NEST suggested some votes for the Communists and New People could be quiet protest against economic strain. With a supermajority secured, the Kremlin faces no parliamentary obstacle as it continues the war, now well into its fifth year.

Why it matters

The result locks in Kremlin control of Russia's legislature for the coming years, leaving no domestic institutional check on the war in Ukraine.
