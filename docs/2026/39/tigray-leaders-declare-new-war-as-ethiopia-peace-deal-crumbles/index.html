Tigray leaders declare new war as Ethiopia peace deal crumbles

Tigrayan forces seized airports and fought in neighbouring Afar and Amhara, threatening the 2022 Pretoria Agreement.

Leaders in Ethiopia's Tigray region said on Wednesday they had entered a new war with the federal government, describing it as defensive, as fighting broke out in Tigray and the neighbouring Afar and Amhara regions. Getachew Reda, an adviser to Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, said Tigrayan forces had taken federally held airports and launched an all-out offensive. Ethiopian Airlines suspended flights to the region. Days earlier, the TPLF had announced an alliance with six other armed groups aiming to topple Abiy.

The 2020 to 2022 Tigray war killed hundreds of thousands of people before the Pretoria Agreement. The government accuses Eritrea of funding the alliance, which Eritrea has denied. The African Union called for immediate de-escalation, and the US ambassador called the offensive an unwinnable fight.

Why it matters

A renewed war in Africa's second most populous country could pull in Eritrea and destabilise the entire Horn of Africa.
