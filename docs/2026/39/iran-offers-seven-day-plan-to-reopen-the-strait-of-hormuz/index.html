Iran offers seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

Tehran says it would reopen the vital waterway and restart nuclear talks within a week if the US lifts its blockade, waives oil sanctions and agrees a wider ceasefire.

Iran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear negotiations within seven days if the United States meets a set of conditions, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. He outlined the plan at a private gathering in New York on Thursday, and it was widely reported on Friday. The proposal was passed to Washington through mediators, with Qatar relaying messages.

According to the AP, Iran's terms are that the US lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales and observe a ceasefire that includes Lebanon. Al Jazeera reported that the clock would start the day after the US accepts, with the strait reopening at the end of the seventh day and talks resuming after that.

The plan closely resembles the memorandum of understanding signed on 17 June, which set a 60-day window of toll-free shipping and negotiations before collapsing amid renewed attacks. Iran's new version speeds up the timeline and changes the order of steps. Araghchi described his meeting earlier in the week with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner as productive.

Washington was cautious. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US wants Iran's nuclear programme dealt with immediately as part of one package and warned against calling recent contacts a breakthrough. Trita Parsi of the Quincy Institute, who attended the gathering, said Araghchi pitched a fast deal as helpful to Trump before the midterms, while noting suspicion that Iran might be buying time to sell more oil.

Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera doubted Washington would revive the June framework, arguing the US believes it can outlast Iran economically. Pezeshkian hosted Qatar's prime minister early on Friday as mediation continued.

Why it matters

Reopening Hormuz would ease a squeeze on global oil and gas supplies that has fuelled inflation worldwide for seven months.
