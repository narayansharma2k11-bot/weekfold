Typhoon Dujuan kills at least four around Tokyo

Record rainfall triggered landslides and left tens of thousands of homes without power in Chiba as the storm swept past the Japanese capital.

Typhoon Dujuan killed at least four people in the Tokyo region, three in Chiba prefecture and one in Kanagawa, according to Japan's Fire and Disaster Management Agency. Six people were missing and 22 injured after torrential rain set off landslides. More than 45,000 households in Chiba were still without electricity on Tuesday afternoon, and over 460 homes were damaged.

NHK reported 832mm of rain over 48 hours on Oshima island, more than twice its usual September total, while Tokyo's Edogawa ward set a 24-hour record of 282.5mm. The storm disrupted trains and roads and forced a major video game fair in Tokyo to close early before moving out over the Pacific.

Why it matters

Extreme rainfall hitting one of the world's largest metropolitan areas shows how intensifying storms test even well-prepared cities.
