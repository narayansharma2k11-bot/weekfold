Hurricane Polo becomes one of the strongest Pacific storms on record

The Category 5 hurricane reached 180mph winds off Mexico's southwest coast, forcing port closures in Guerrero and Michoacan.

Hurricane Polo strengthened into a Category 5 storm with maximum sustained winds of 180mph (290km/h) off Mexico's Pacific coast, the US National Hurricane Center said, citing a NOAA Hurricane Hunter flight. Colorado State University's Phil Klotzbach said it tied the strongest Northern Hemisphere storms of 2026 and ranked second by pressure among recorded eastern Pacific systems, behind 2015's Hurricane Patricia. It was the season's third Category 5 Pacific hurricane.

Forecasters expected the compact storm to stay offshore and turn northwest, but warned of heavy rain, dangerous surf and mudslides. Mexico's navy closed ports in Guerrero and Michoacan, and President Claudia Sheinbaum urged coastal residents to stay alert.

Why it matters

The rapid intensification of Pacific hurricanes is a growing risk for coastal communities and ports along Mexico's busy Pacific shipping routes.
