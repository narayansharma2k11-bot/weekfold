Trump rejects Iran's plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

Calling the seven-day offer unacceptable, Trump said Iran wanted a quick deal because it was losing badly, dimming hopes of an early end to the war.

President Donald Trump on Saturday rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, telling reporters outside the White House it would not be acceptable. He said Tehran wanted an immediate opening because it was losing badly. Early that morning he had posted an image on Truth Social labelling the waterway the Trump Strait.

Under the plan set out by Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, Iran would restore normal shipping within a week if the US lifted its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waived sanctions on Iranian oil sales and observed a ceasefire covering Lebanon. Araghchi said Iran wanted nothing beyond the terms of the June memorandum of understanding and that the choice now lay with Washington.

The rejection followed a week of diplomacy at the UN, including three hours of indirect talks on 22 September, the first such round since the June deal collapsed. A White House official described talks through mediators as positive and constructive but said the US was in a strong position and in no hurry. Rubio said any deal would require a lot of hard work over time.

The Wall Street Journal, citing US officials, reported that Trump had told aides a renewed bombing campaign was likely after the midterms, though officials said that could change. For Iran, lifting the blockade and sanctions would relieve an economy suffering record-low currency values and soaring inflation. For the US, reopening the strait would calm markets and reduce the burden on naval forces escorting ships.

Analyst Danny Citrinowicz of Israel's Institute for National Security Studies said Washington may believe time is on its side, while Tehran may conclude the opposite. Pezeshkian has returned to Tehran, according to Iranian media.

Why it matters

The rejection prolongs the closure of a chokepoint for about a fifth of global oil and gas trade and raises the risk of renewed strikes.
