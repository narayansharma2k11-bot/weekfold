Merz's CDU suffers historic defeats in two German state elections

The chancellor's party collapsed in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and lost Berlin to the Left, while the far-right AfD surged.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democrats suffered heavy losses in state elections on Sunday. In Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, the CDU fell to about 4.9 per cent, below the 5 per cent threshold, while the far-right AfD came first with 38.2 per cent, ahead of the Social Democrats on 35.5 per cent. In Berlin, the Left party won with 25.7 per cent, the CDU dropped to 18.8 per cent from 28.2 per cent in 2023, and the AfD rose to 16.3 per cent.

Merz, 70 and in office for 16 months, called the outcome a disaster but said he would stay on as party leader and chancellor until the end of his term, insisting his economic reforms must go ahead. He cancelled a planned trip to the UN General Assembly. Despite its gains, the AfD has no realistic path into government in either state because other parties refuse to work with it.

Why it matters

Weakness at the top of Europe's largest economy, and a strengthening far right, affect EU policy on Ukraine, migration and economic reform.
