Climate · Saturday 26 September 2026

All of Bangkok declared a disaster zone after days of rain

Nearly 300mm of rain flooded roads and homes across the Thai capital, prompting a disaster declaration in all 50 districts.

Bangkok authorities declared all 50 of the city's districts disaster zones on Saturday after almost 300mm of rain fell from Thursday afternoon, flooding roads, homes and shops. Governor Chadchart Sittipunt said canals were full and warned residents near them, while about 980 people moved into temporary shelters. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul cut short a trip to London.

The city said pumping stations were at full capacity and draining could take two to three days once the rain stopped. Bangkok had recorded 487mm of rain from 1 to 25 September, against an average of about 269mm. One canalside vendor told reporters the flooding was worse than in 2011, when nationwide floods killed more than 500 people.

Why it matters

Flooding in a low-lying megacity of millions shows the growing climate risk to Asia's economic hubs and supply chains.
