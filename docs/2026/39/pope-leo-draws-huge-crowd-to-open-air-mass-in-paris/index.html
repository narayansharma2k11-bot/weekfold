Pope Leo draws huge crowd to open-air Mass in Paris

Hundreds of thousands filled the Champs-Elysees and Place de la Concorde on the first official papal visit to France in nearly two decades.

Pope Leo XIV celebrated an open-air Mass at the Place de la Concorde in Paris on Saturday after travelling down the Champs-Elysees in his popemobile. Vatican crowd estimates were reported at between about 700,000 (AFP) and 800,000 (AP). Central Paris was closed to traffic. The four-day trip began on Friday with prayers at the restored Notre Dame, the first papal visit since the 2019 fire, and a youth vigil.

In his homily, Leo urged believers to share their faith. During the visit he also warned against an AI-driven paradise of machines and called on France to champion peace. Adult baptisms in France have roughly tripled since 2016, though overall numbers remain far below past levels. The pope travelled on to Lourdes.

Why it matters

The first US-born pope is using major foreign visits to push the Church's message on AI ethics, migration and peace.
