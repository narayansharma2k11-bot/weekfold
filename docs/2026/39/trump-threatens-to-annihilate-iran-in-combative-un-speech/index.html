Trump threatens to annihilate Iran in combative UN speech

At the UN General Assembly, the US president demanded Iran's economic isolation and predicted a deal only after November's midterms, as Iran's delegation walked out.

US President Donald Trump used his address to the 81st UN General Assembly in New York on Tuesday to defend the war with Iran and again threaten the Islamic Republic with destruction. He framed his choice as one between a deal and moving to wipe out the Iranian state quickly, and he called for the complete economic isolation of Iran. Members of Iran's delegation walked out while he spoke.

Trump accused Tehran of stalling on a deal to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz until after the US midterm elections in November. He predicted an agreement would come shortly after the vote, while insisting the election played no part in his own thinking. He also repeated that he would never allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon.

The war began on 28 February, when the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran. A memorandum of understanding signed in June collapsed within weeks after Iran resumed attacks on tankers, and US strikes restarted. The US now blockades Iranian ports and escorts commercial ships. Before the war, about a fifth of the world's oil and gas shipments passed through Hormuz; National Review reported that US gasoline prices have risen by nearly half since the conflict began.

Hours before the speech, a senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran could reopen the strait within seven days if Washington eased military pressure and lifted the blockade. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf said Trump could not impose his will on Iranians. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US remained open to talks that could produce results.

After the speech, Trump met Gulf leaders from Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, who want shipping restored but are wary of empowering Iran. Qatar has floated a regional security framework that Iran is said to be reviewing.

Why it matters

The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important energy chokepoint, so the war's direction drives fuel prices and inflation around the globe.
