Trump hosts Xi at White House, extends trade truce to January

Xi's first Washington visit in more than a decade brought lavish ceremony, a two-month trade extension and sharp differences on Taiwan and AI.

President Donald Trump welcomed Chinese President Xi Jinping to the White House on Thursday for a state visit, Xi's first trip to Washington in over a decade. The programme included a red-carpet arrival at Joint Base Andrews, a Rose Garden ceremony with a military flyover and a state dinner whose guests reportedly included Tim Cook, Sundar Pichai, Sam Altman and Jensen Huang.

The main concrete outcome was on trade. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two sides agreed to extend their trade truce, which was due to lapse in November, by two months to 10 January. Xi said a new trade arrangement had been reached and called it good news for the global economy. Bessent said China had met its pledge to buy 25 million tonnes of soybeans but lagged on other farm purchases, while rare-earth deliveries were also falling short.

On Taiwan, Chinese state media said Xi urged Washington to handle the issue with prudence and to oppose Taiwanese independence, a stronger stance than the long-standing US position of not supporting it. The White House issued no immediate readout. Xi also called for regular military dialogue and better crisis-prevention tools.

The leaders diverged on artificial intelligence. Xi said both countries had a responsibility to keep AI under human control, while Trump posted that he wanted to leave the technology largely where it is, without new guardrails. They also discussed the Korean peninsula, the Middle East and the war in Ukraine; Xinhua said Xi backed a return to a US-Iran ceasefire.

US television networks did not carry the leaders' remarks live in protest at the White House ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico. Trump and Xi are expected to meet again at APEC in Shenzhen and the G20 in Miami.

Why it matters

Relations between the world's two largest economies shape global trade, technology supply chains and security in the Taiwan Strait.
