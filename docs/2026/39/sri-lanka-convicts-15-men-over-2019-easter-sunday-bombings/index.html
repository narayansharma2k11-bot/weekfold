Sri Lanka convicts 15 men over 2019 Easter Sunday bombings

After a trial lasting more than five years, a Colombo court found 15 men guilty of roles in attacks that killed more than 260 people.

A court in Colombo on Tuesday convicted 15 men for their part in organising the April 2019 Easter Sunday bombings, in which suicide attackers struck three churches and three luxury hotels. The attacks, inspired by the Islamic State group and blamed on two local militant groups, killed more than 260 people, including 42 foreigners, and wounded around 500. The men were sentenced to life in prison; Sri Lanka has not carried out an execution since 1976.

Charges were filed in 2021 against 24 suspects, covering murder, terrorism conspiracy and weapons offences. None of the bombers survived. In July, a former police chief and a former senior defence official were sentenced to death for ignoring foreign intelligence warnings. Sri Lanka's Catholic Church maintains, without offering evidence, that the true masterminds have not been identified.

Why it matters

The verdicts are a milestone in one of South Asia's deadliest terror attacks, which shook Sri Lanka's tourism economy and its religious communities.
