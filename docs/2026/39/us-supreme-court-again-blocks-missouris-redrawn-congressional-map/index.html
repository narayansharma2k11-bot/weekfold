US Supreme Court again blocks Missouri's redrawn congressional map

For the third time, the justices refused to let Missouri use a 2025 map designed to win Republicans an extra House seat in November.

The US Supreme Court on Friday paused an appeals court ruling that would have required Missouri to use a congressional map drawn in 2025, meaning the state's 2022 districts will be used in November's midterm elections. The unsigned order, with no public dissents, said switching maps after the election had begun would cause chaos. It was the third time the court had intervened.

The new map aimed to give Republicans seven of Missouri's eight House seats by carving up Democrat Emanuel Cleaver's Kansas City district. Missouri's Supreme Court ruled that a citizen referendum petition with more than 300,000 signatures blocks the map unless voters approve it. Governor Mike Kehoe called the decision disappointing; Cleaver welcomed it.

Why it matters

Mid-decade redistricting battles could decide which party controls the US House, shaping Washington's policy on trade, Iran and Ukraine.
