Iran's navy strikes tanker in Strait of Hormuz amid shipping chaos

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed responsibility for striking a third tanker in two days in the Strait of Hormuz, as cargo ships face mounting attacks threatening global trade.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said its forces struck another cargo ship in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the third vessel hit by Iranian forces in two days. Crew members were reportedly safe, but the incident underscores escalating attacks on commercial shipping in one of the world's most critical chokepoints for global trade.

The tanker attacks, along with parallel Houthi strikes on shipping and Saudi infrastructure, have sent shockwaves through global energy markets. Oil prices have risen sharply, and shipping companies are chartering more expensive routes around Africa to avoid the conflict zone.

Yemen's Houthi movement, backed by Iran, warned the United States of a swift and painful response if American forces joined Saudi-led operations in Houthi-held territory. Their strikes on civilian vessels and Saudi airports have killed civilians and disrupted regional commerce. Both sides show no sign of backing down.

The attacks come as the Trump administration claims both sides agreed to halt strikes on energy infrastructure, a claim both Russia and Ukraine denied. The chaos in the Middle East has further complicated efforts to stabilize global energy supplies and shipping lanes.

Why it matters

Attacks on global shipping threaten the flow of oil and goods worldwide, with potential to deepen a global energy and economic crisis.
