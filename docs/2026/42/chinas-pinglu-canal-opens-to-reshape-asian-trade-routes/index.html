China's Pinglu Canal opens to reshape Asian trade routes

China inaugurated the $11 billion Pinglu Canal, a 134-kilometer waterway linking southwestern China to the Beibu Gulf and Southeast Asia, shortening shipping times and reducing costs for the region's largest trade partner.

China opened its Pinglu Canal to commercial navigation on September 16, marking completion of one of the world's most ambitious infrastructure projects. The 134-kilometer waterway, which stretches from Nanning to Qinzhou in Guangxi province, creates a second maritime route to the sea for southwestern China and directly links the country's inland cities to the Beibu Gulf and Southeast Asia.

The canal cost approximately 11 billion dollars to build and required four years of construction beginning in August 2022. It accommodates cargo vessels up to 5,000 tons and includes three cascading ship locks designed to minimize water use. On its opening day, 14 loaded cargo ships passed through the locks, with 16 more carrying bulk goods bound for the Xijiang River Basin.

The waterway is expected to save shipping companies over 560 kilometers of travel compared to existing routes and projected to save regions along the route more than 5 billion yuan annually in freight costs. Four new trade routes opened between Nanning and Yangpu in Hainan, as well as between Nanning and Can Tho in Vietnam.

China has framed the project as essential infrastructure supporting regional cooperation with Southeast Asia, its largest trading partner. The canal follows in the tradition of ancient waterways like the Lingqu Canal, built over 2,000 years ago in the same region, and represents a modern effort to reshape Asian trade logistics in China's favor.

Why it matters

The canal gives China a direct link to Southeast Asia and the Beibu Gulf, strengthening its economic ties and strategic position in the region while reshaping global shipping routes.
