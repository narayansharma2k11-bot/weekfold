Conflict · Monday 12 October 2026

Houthis strike Saudi airport for third time in as many days

A Houthi missile hit the passenger terminal at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Monday, killing one person and forcing operations to suspend after a strike a day earlier left at least 12 dead.

The Houthi missile hit a passenger terminal at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Monday morning, killing one person and wounding several others in a strike that forced Saudi authorities to suspend airport operations. The attack was the third in as many days; a similar strike on Sunday left at least 12 people dead at the same facility.

The Iran-backed Houthis have intensified attacks on Saudi civilian infrastructure as part of a broader escalation of Middle East conflict. The group has fired dozens of missiles and drones at the kingdom's airports, oil facilities and shipping in the Red Sea over recent months. Saudi officials vowed a serious response, but the airport remained shut as of Monday evening.

The Pentagon separately revealed it has quietly raised its official count of U.S. service members killed in the widening Middle East conflict to 21, up from 18 reported earlier. The increase reflects intensifying operations across the region involving Iran, Saudi Arabia, the United States and their respective allies. The broader conflict has also killed and displaced thousands of civilians in Yemen, Gaza and across the region.

Seventy-four Democrats in Congress sent a letter to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth pressing him for details on civilian deaths from U.S. military strikes. One strike in September targeted a wedding in Yemen, killing dozens. Hegseth has called for continued military operations, despite the reported toll on non-combatants and rising fuel prices as attacks disrupt global shipping.

Why it matters

Attacks on civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia and worldwide airspace near the Strait of Hormuz are destabilizing oil markets, disrupting trade, and pulling the U.S. deeper into Middle East conflict.
