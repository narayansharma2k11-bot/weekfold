Conflict · Thursday 15 October 2026

Russia intensifies Ukraine attacks as Trump claim of ceasefire falls apart

Russian drone strikes killed at least nine civilians in Ukraine on Wednesday, leaving hundreds of thousands without power. President Zelensky said he felt betrayed by Trump's announcement of an energy ceasefire neither side supports.

Russian forces launched a new wave of drone strikes on Ukrainian cities Wednesday, killing at least nine civilians and destroying critical energy infrastructure. The attacks left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without electricity as winter approaches.

The strikes came just days after President Trump announced an 'energy ceasefire' intended to spare civilian power plants from attack. Both Russia and Ukraine immediately denied agreeing to any such terms. Zelensky said Trump's announcement caught him off guard and accused the U.S. president of misrepresenting the conflict's status.

Ukraine responded by resuming strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, escalating the tit-for-tat destruction of civilian power systems. The escalation underscores the fragility of any ceasefire effort in a conflict where both sides view energy infrastructure as legitimate military targets.

The strikes coincide with Trump's push for negotiations to end the war. Critics argue that without pressure on Russia to accept Ukrainian terms, negotiations risk codifying Russian territorial gains. Ukraine has demanded the restoration of its 1991 borders before agreeing to talks.

Why it matters

Continued attacks on energy infrastructure threaten to plunge Ukraine into a humanitarian crisis as winter sets in, while diplomatic efforts stumble.
