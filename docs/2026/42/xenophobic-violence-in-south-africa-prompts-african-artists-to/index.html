Xenophobic violence in South Africa prompts African artists to withdraw from performances

Escalating attacks on migrants in South Africa prompted prominent African musicians to cancel or reschedule performances, including Malian artist Salif Keita and South African singer Tyla.

Xenophobic violence in South Africa has prompted leading African musicians to withdraw from planned performances. Malian musician Salif Keita canceled a September concert, citing 'reports of xenophobic violence.' South African singer Tyla removed Lagos from her tour dates following a boycott petition in Nigeria, and Zimbabwean festival organizers dropped South African amapiano star Musa Keys.

The escalation of xenophobic violence reflects long-standing tensions in South Africa over immigration and economic opportunity. Foreign nationals, particularly from other African countries, face harassment, attacks and displacement. At least 1,300 xenophobic incidents have been recorded since 1994, with over 690 deaths and approximately 128,000 displacements.

In early 2026, at least five Ethiopians were shot dead in Johannesburg by the end of April. Police are investigating, but no official report has conclusively linked the killings to xenophobia. Ethiopian community leaders report widespread intimidation and exclusion.

The withdrawal of artists from South African venues sends a message of solidarity with migrants and opposition to xenophobic violence. Cultural boycotts have historically played a role in movements for social change, raising pressure on South African authorities to address the crisis and protect vulnerable migrants.

Why it matters

Xenophobic violence threatens the safety of millions of migrants and undermines the principle of free movement within Africa.
